# GEB v2 positioning

## Core contribution

Chapter 1 is a global quantitative trait-geography paper, not a causal-mechanism paper and not a report about two surviving coefficients.

The established novelty is **global continuous within-taxon public-image phenomics**: public biodiversity photographs are treated as repeated quantitative phenotype observations, allowing the analysis to move beyond both species-mean trait databases and coarse morphological categories.

The central route is:

> public photographs -> detected capitula -> continuous trait measurements -> global phenotypic diversity -> within-taxon distributions -> multiscale environment-trait structure

## What v2 changes

PR70's finer continuous-trait contract is adopted as the measurement layer. It extends the original orientation/colour/outline endpoints to finer capitulum modules without replacing the established manuscript logic.

The contract contains 27 continuous endpoints across eight modules. Ecological inference is tiered:

- 9 established primary endpoints;
- 10 continuous candidate endpoints, including visible floral display and finer involucral/armature geometry;
- 4 descriptive colour-composition endpoints;
- 4 validation-only surface/exudate image signals.

The candidate layer adds quantitative definitions for involucre length/width, apical and basal taper, radial projection roughness, upper-tail and maximum projection, outward spread, projection-peak density and bilateral asymmetry. These are image-derived continuous phenotypes until botanical calibration justifies a more specific botanical name.

## Main analytical flow

1. **Global phenotype recovery.** Show that public photographs can yield repeated numerical phenotypes rather than one categorical state per taxon.
2. **Phenotypic geography and scale.** Quantify within- and among-taxon diversity and show how much structure species/taxon means hide.
3. **Within-taxon environment-trait structure.** Use the frozen spatially thinned global cohort as the primary environmental estimand.
4. **Spatial context.** Retain grouped SPDE-INLA for established endpoints; candidate high-resolution traits receive separate image-quality sensitivity rather than being forced into the same data regime.
5. **Among-taxon environmental sorting.** Retain the permutation-based niche layer as a separate ecological scale.
6. **Historical sensitivity.** Keep the incomplete-backbone PGLS layer in Supporting Information rather than treating it as resolved phylogenetic correction.
7. **Robustness grading.** Seasonal, dominant-taxon, hemisphere, native-range, image-quality and repeat-photo checks constrain confidence and interpretation rather than universally vetoing the global pattern.
8. **Mechanistic hypotheses.** Microclimate, precipitation exposure, optical/biotic interactions and antagonist/protection hypotheses belong in Discussion as explanations to test, not as demonstrated causes.

## Evidence hierarchy

- **A — robust:** supported in the global analysis and consistent across the relevant sensitivity layers.
- **B — supported but sensitivity-dependent:** supported in the global/spatial analysis but weakened in a restricted-domain sensitivity while direction remains compatible.
- **C — exploratory continuous signal:** candidate-tier image phenotype with environmental structure but incomplete image-quality and/or botanical calibration.
- **D — unresolved/artifact-prone:** important sign instability or failure of basic measurement robustness.

Native-only restriction is a sensitivity to geographic status, not a replacement estimand. Failure to retain BH support in the restricted native-only subset does not by itself erase a same-signed association in the frozen global cohort.

## Biological story

The manuscript asks how thistle capitulum phenotype is distributed across the world and environmental space. The expected synthesis is not one universal climate-associated thistle syndrome. Instead, capitulum phenotype is treated as a composite set of modules whose spatial structure may differ among orientation, visible colour, gross outline, floral display and finer involucral/armature geometry.

The paper therefore contributes:

1. a scalable image-to-continuous-trait measurement framework;
2. a global description of repeated within-taxon phenotypic diversity;
3. a quantitative map of trait- and scale-specific environmental structure;
4. an evidence-graded set of ecological patterns that can generate later causal and evolutionary tests.

## Claim boundary

V2 does not infer phenotypic plasticity, local adaptation, selection, pollinator causation, antagonist causation, defensive function, secretion function or evolutionary rates from cross-sectional photographs. It also does not relabel unvalidated image proxies as direct botanical measurements.

Those limits define what the pattern means; they do not reduce the manuscript's main contribution to only the associations that survive every possible sensitivity analysis.
