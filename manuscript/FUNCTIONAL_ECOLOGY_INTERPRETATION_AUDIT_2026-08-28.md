# Functional-ecology interpretation audit for the two retained GEB candidates

Date: 2026-08-28
Scope: primary research only; no change to the frozen results or claim ceiling

## Executive decision

The two retained among-taxon associations can be presented as a second, ecological contribution because both occupy functional trait-environment axes established independently in primary studies. The strength of that statement differs between the two traits.

- **Orientation-annual precipitation is directionally coherent with prior functional ecology.** Comparative data show more down-facing flowers toward wetter environments, and manipulative studies show that nodding or pendulous presentation can reduce rain damage to pollen and improve reproductive output. This is a strong rationale for a rain-shielding hypothesis, but the Azami measurement is relative to EXIF-image vertical rather than gravity and does not itself measure rain interception, pollen or fitness.
- **Chroma-radiation is coherent only with a broad radiation-responsive pigmentation hypothesis.** UV and light can alter floral anthocyanin accumulation, and high-UV source environments can covary with floral anthocyanin concentration. However, camera-derived CIELAB chroma is not a monotonic or pigment-specific proxy for anthocyanin amount. The manuscript must not state that lower chroma generally means more anthocyanin. The observed sign is compatible with some high-concentration anthocyanin systems, but is not diagnostic of them.
- Passing the declared multiplicity, sampling-composition, broad-space/residual-spatial and historical-placement gates makes the two associations unusually well-screened **patterns**. It does not establish the proposed mechanisms, adaptive value in thistles, phylogenetic independence, repeated origins or selection.

## Evidence classification

### 1. Does lower visible CIELAB chroma imply more anthocyanin?

**Verdict: not as a general bridge. The relationship is pigment-, concentration-, pH-, tissue- and acquisition-dependent and can be non-monotonic.**

#### Directly supported

1. In natural *Penstemon* hybrids measured with standardized visible reflectance and matched pigment chemistry, total anthocyanin mass fraction contributed **positively** to chroma; pigment composition affected hue, and complete reflectance spectra inferred pigment quantities better than chroma, brightness and hue summaries. This is evidence against a general rule that less chroma means more anthocyanin. The controlled model predicted chroma with mean test-set R2 = 0.78 and inferred total pigment from the spectral summaries with R2 = 0.67. [Stevens et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1093/icb/icad073)

2. In black dahlias, purified cyanidin- and pelargonidin-based anthocyanins showed a **two-phase** CIELAB C* response to increasing concentration: C* first increased, then decreased beyond an inflection at high concentration. The effect depended on anthocyanin structure and pH. High cyanidin accumulation could therefore contribute to low C* in this particular high-concentration, black-flower system, but the authors explicitly showed that cyanidin-rich systems do not always lower C*. This supplies a specific compatibility example, not a general conversion rule for purple thistle corollas. [Deguchi et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.2503/hortj.MI-121)

3. In 44 *Lycoris longituba* variants, higher total anthocyanin was associated mainly with lower lightness and, in red and orange groups, greater redness; relationships varied among hue groups, and carotenoids, co-pigmentation and pH also influenced visible colour. C* covaried with lightness differently across colour groups. [He et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0022098)

4. Calibrated digital images can estimate anthocyanin concentration, but the validated predictors were bespoke RGB/reflectance indices such as strength of green and anthocyanin-content ratios, not uncalibrated CIELAB C*. Performance differed among species and tissues. [del Valle et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1002/ece3.3804)

#### Inferentially compatible

The negative Azami chroma-radiation association may motivate tests of radiation-responsive changes in pigment amount, pigment composition, co-pigmentation or petal optics. The dahlia experiment shows that low C* can occur at high anthocyanin concentration in a restricted high-concentration state, so that mechanism is possible in principle. It is not identified in the present thistle data.

#### Unsupported and should not appear as a result

- “Lower chroma means higher anthocyanin concentration.”
- “Higher radiation caused anthocyanin accumulation, which caused lower chroma.”
- “CIELAB chroma is an anthocyanin assay” or “a UV-reflectance measure.”

### 2. Does solar or UV radiation promote floral anthocyanin regulation?

**Verdict: radiation responsiveness is directly supported, but a universal positive response is not.**

#### Directly supported

1. UV-exclusion and supplementation experiments in two rose cultivars showed suppressed petal anthocyanin accumulation without UV-B and renewed accumulation after UV-B exposure. The response involved cyanidin glycosides and was strongest around 50% anthesis, so it is both taxon- and developmental-stage-specific. This directly supports the existing phrase “UV-B can induce petal anthocyanin accumulation.” [Hennayake et al. 2006](https://doi.org/10.2525/ecb.44.103)

2. A UV-exclusion experiment in *Silene littorea* reduced petal anthocyanin concentration by about 20%. UV exposure simultaneously reduced pollen production by 31% and total seed production per plant by 69%, demonstrating that pigment induction is not equivalent to a net fitness benefit. [del Valle et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0231611)

3. Greenhouse-raised *Clarkia unguiculata* populations sourced from high-solar-UV sites had higher estimated floral anthocyanin concentration, providing a geographic differentiation precedent. However, supplemental UV treatment within the greenhouse had a significant **negative** effect on estimated anthocyanin, the solar-UV slope depended on latitude, and anthocyanin concentration did not predict pollen performance. The paper supports environmental responsiveness and geographic covariance, not a universal positive causal response or demonstrated adaptation. [Peach et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpls.2020.00847)

4. Attached petunia flowers required light for chalcone-synthase expression and anthocyanin accumulation, but visible wavelengths rather than UV-A or UV-B supplied the effective signal. This is a direct counterexample to a universal UV-specific pathway. [Katz & Weiss 1999](https://doi.org/10.1080/07929978.1999.10676777)

#### Safe synthesis

Primary studies support **radiation-responsive floral pigment regulation** and **geographic differentiation of floral pigmentation across radiative environments**. They do not identify the pigment basis or direction of the Azami CIELAB coefficient. A calibrated visible/UV reflectance pass, petal chemistry or expression assays and a common-garden light manipulation remain the decisive tests.

### 3. Does downward or nodding orientation protect reproductive structures from rain?

**Verdict: directly supported in several species and directionally matched by an independent precipitation-gradient study; still context-dependent.**

#### Directly supported

1. In the nodding Asteraceae *Cremanthodium campanulatum*, investigators tethered capitula upright before anthesis. Natural nodding capitula had 56.3% achene set versus 15.7% in artificially erect capitula, while visitation did not differ. Water and UV-B reduced pollen germination, and the involucral bracts sharply reduced UV-B exposure. This is the closest taxonomic and architectural precedent for the thistle hypothesis. The experiment supports the integrated nodding-capitulum shelter phenotype, not angle alone. [Chen et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1080/17550874.2012.702793)

2. In *Anisodus luridus*, manipulative orientation experiments showed that artificial erection increased pollen loss after rainwash; pendulous flowers protected pollen and later upright fruit orientation facilitated fertilized-ovule development. [Wang et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.3732/ajb.1000010)

3. Across 80 angiosperm species, water contact greatly shortened pollen longevity, and species whose flower structures completely protected pollen had water-susceptible pollen. This supports the general functional hypothesis that floral structures shelter rain-susceptible pollen, while also showing alternative strategies such as water-resistant pollen and delayed presentation. [Mao & Huang 2009](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1469-8137.2009.02925.x)

4. In southern South American forests, genera with down-facing flowers increased along a strong Andean precipitation gradient irrespective of pollination mode. This is an unusually close ecological-geographic precedent for the Azami orientation-annual-precipitation direction, although it is a cross-flora comparative association rather than a thistle experiment. [Aizen 2003](https://doi.org/10.2307/3647342)

#### Important negative/context evidence

An orientation manipulation in the hummingbird-pollinated mistletoe *Tristerix corymbosus* did not support flower-flooding or pollen-protection explanations; rain diluted nectar independently of orientation, and orientation did not affect pollen remaining or seed set. Rain protection is therefore architecture- and species-dependent, not a universal property of downward flowers. [Tadey & Aizen 2001](https://doi.org/10.1046/j.0269-8463.2001.00580.x)

#### Safe synthesis

The Azami direction is **consistent with a rain-shielding functional hypothesis demonstrated elsewhere**, especially in another nodding Asteraceae, and it independently resembles the Aizen precipitation-gradient pattern. It is not evidence that the photographed thistle capitula were gravity-referenced, intercepted less rain or had higher fitness.

## How the robustness evidence should be described

The frozen candidate rows support the following exact statement:

- Chroma-shortwave radiation: among-taxon standardized beta = -0.3454, global BH q = 0.006; broad-space beta = -0.7124, permutation p = 0.001; residual Moran p = 0.217; direction retained in all declared sampling perturbations, with minimum effect-magnitude ratio 0.6149; direction and p < 0.05 retained on 52/52 historical-placement trees.
- Orientation-annual precipitation: among-taxon standardized beta = 0.3044, global BH q = 0.021; broad-space beta = 0.2861, permutation p = 0.017; residual Moran p = 0.082; direction retained in all declared sampling perturbations, with minimum effect-magnitude ratio 0.7808; direction and p < 0.05 retained on 52/52 historical-placement trees.

These results justify saying that the two associations remained directionally stable under the **tested** sampling-composition perturbations, broad spatial basis, residual spatial diagnostic and historical-placement alternatives. Avoid stronger substitutions:

- say “no residual spatial autocorrelation was detected by the declared diagnostic,” not “spatial structure was removed”;
- say “historical-placement sensitivity passed,” not “phylogenetic constraint was excluded” or “the associations are phylogenetically independent”;
- say “sampling direction was stable,” not “sampling bias was corrected” or “the sample is globally representative”;
- say “adaptive-pattern candidates under current controls,” not “adaptive traits,” “local adaptation,” “convergence” or “independent origins.”

## Recommended Discussion wording

> The second contribution is ecological. After global multiplicity control, declared sampling-composition perturbations, broad-space and residual-spatial diagnostics, and 52 alternative historical placements, two among-taxon associations remained: lower photographed corolla chroma in higher-radiation environments and a larger image-referenced head-axis angle in wetter environments. Both occupy functional axes established independently in floral ecology, but the strength of interpretation differs. UV and light can regulate petal anthocyanins, and floral pigmentation can vary geographically with solar UV; moreover, high-concentration anthocyanin systems can show non-monotonic chroma responses (Hennayake et al. 2006; Deguchi et al. 2016; del Valle et al. 2020; Peach et al. 2020). Nevertheless, CIELAB chroma is neither a monotonic nor pigment-specific proxy for anthocyanin concentration, so the observed sign does not identify pigment amount or pathway mediation. By contrast, the direction of the orientation-precipitation association agrees with comparative increases in down-facing flowers along a precipitation gradient and with manipulations showing rain-shielding and reproductive benefits of nodding presentation, including in another Asteraceae (Aizen 2003; Mao & Huang 2009; Wang et al. 2010; Chen et al. 2013). These precedents make the two rows functionally interpretable adaptive-pattern candidates, not demonstrations that radiation or rain caused the present thistle distributions or that the measured traits are adaptations.
