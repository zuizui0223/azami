# Methods

## Study design and cohorts

We used public biodiversity photographs to quantify continuous capitulum traits across *Cirsium* and allied thistles while retaining repeated observations within source-assigned taxa. Source names were treated as operational taxonomic units, rather than as a resolved genus-wide taxonomy, and authority-backed lumping was evaluated separately. Cohort definitions and counts are given in Table 1 and Table S1.1; geographic coverage and filtering are shown in Figure 2 and Figures S1.6 and S1.7.

Two data streams answered complementary questions. A balanced image atlas contained 3,725 observations, 6,626 detected capitula and 216 taxa. Each observation contributed one photograph, which could contain multiple heads. This layer supported trait summaries, nested visible-variance decomposition, taxon-level principal components analysis and among-taxon analyses. An exhaustive stream applied detection and measurement before trait-based thinning. It contained 406,582 detector-positive observations from 286 taxa. Coordinate filtering retained 392,989 observations from 271 taxa; a positional-accuracy threshold of ≤10 km retained 297,293 observations from 259 taxa; and one observation per taxon × 0.25° cell yielded the primary cohort of 46,276 observations from 259 taxa. Statistical families from these cohorts were analysed separately.

## Detection and continuous image measurements

Traits were selected when they were repeatedly visible, reproducibly measurable, explicitly assessable or unassessable, and complementary in biological interpretation (Table 2; Table S1.2). Fine indumentum, glandularity, mucilage, absolute size without scale and whole-plant characters were excluded rather than coded as absent.

Visible capitula were localized with a single-class You Only Look Once version 11 nano (YOLO11n) detector initialized from `yolo11n.pt` and trained for 40 epochs at 640-pixel input size on 270 pseudo-labelled images. The confidence threshold was 0.25. Detection defined regions of interest only; downstream traits were calculated by deterministic image functions. Source observation and photograph identifiers, bounding boxes and contextual crops were retained.

Nine primary endpoints represented three modules: orientation angle relative to Exchangeable Image File Format (EXIF)-oriented image vertical; visible corolla colour as Commission Internationale de l'Éclairage L*a*b* (CIELAB) lightness, chroma and sine/cosine components of circular hue; and outline aspect ratio, circularity, solidity and width-profile coefficient of variation. Image vertical is a reproducible reference, not a direct inclinometer measurement of gravity. Hue components were interpreted jointly. Failed or unstable measurements remained missing. Horizontal mirroring provided a technical repeatability check. In the atlas, 5,777 heads were usable for colour, 5,324 for outline and 4,585 for orientation.

A detector-audit packet was sampled after excluding every photograph and observation used in detector development. It comprised 1,000 images from 323 species and 85 ten-degree spatial blocks; 250 images were assigned to a second annotator. Adjudicated human boxes are incomplete, so detector precision and recall are not used as biological evidence. Independent reference measurements for continuous traits are likewise required to quantify biological accuracy; retention rates and mirror repeatability do not provide that validation.

## High-resolution involucral architecture

A minimum detected-head dimension of 150 pixels selected 1,819 heads from 1,595 photographs and 214 taxa. After resolution, sharpness, segmentation and mirror checks, 1,443 heads from 1,292 observations and 210 taxa were usable. Three auxiliary endpoints were analysed: involucre projection roughness, outward-positive radial-projection fraction and maximum spine-like projection relative to head radius. These measurements describe two-dimensional contour geometry and cannot distinguish longer bract tips from spreading or recurved bracts; they are not direct measures of defensive spine length, recurvature or stiffness.

## Visible variation within and among taxa

For every assessable primary endpoint, total visible image sums of squares were partitioned among taxon means, among photographs within taxa and among heads within photographs. Taxa were resampled as clusters 2,000 times for uncertainty intervals. Two sensitivities retained one deterministic head per photograph or repeatedly sampled 10 photographs per eligible taxon across 500 balanced replicates (Table S1.4; Figure S1.1). Taxon medians for all nine endpoints were standardized and analysed by principal components analysis (PCA). These components describe visible image variance, not genetic variance.

## Within-taxon environmental associations

Four CHELSA v2.1 predictors represented annual mean temperature (BIO1), temperature seasonality (BIO4), annual precipitation (BIO12) and precipitation seasonality (BIO15) for 1981–2010. In the primary cohort, traits and predictors were demeaned within taxon and then standardized. Ordinary least-squares (OLS) coefficients were fitted without an intercept using taxon-clustered standard errors. Benjamini–Hochberg (BH) false-discovery-rate correction was applied across 36 endpoint-component models (Table S1.3; Figure S1.2).

Each primary endpoint was also fitted with Gaussian stochastic partial differential equation models implemented in integrated nested Laplace approximation (SPDE-INLA). Four predefined groups contained climate, climate plus topography, climate plus soil, or all predictors. Models included a taxon random intercept and Matérn spatial field, and each endpoint used the same complete cases across groups. Posterior two-sided tail areas were BH-adjusted globally and within groups; coefficient direction and 95% credible intervals were compared across groups (Table S1.5; Figure S1.3).

### Predeclared image and sampling bias controls

Before inspecting the additional outcomes, we froze the rules in `analysis/ch1/bias_control_contract.json`. First, for all nine endpoints × four CHELSA predictors, both outcome and predictor were residualized within each taxon on an intercept plus sine and cosine of observation day-of-year. The resulting 36 cyclic-season models used taxon-clustered standard errors and a separate BH family. Second, omission taxa were selected only by observation count: each of the ten largest taxa was removed separately, and the two largest taxa were removed jointly. All 36 baseline and cyclic-season models were rerun in every omission scenario (Table S1.11).

Because calendar day represents opposite seasons across hemispheres, a second contract was frozen before inspecting its outcomes (`analysis/ch1/hemisphere_season_sensitivity_contract.json`). We fitted two additional 36-model families. The first added one half-cycle to Southern Hemisphere dates before taxon-specific sine/cosine residualization. The second fitted separate within-taxon intercept and sine/cosine curves for Northern and Southern Hemisphere observations when a taxon occurred in both. The same taxon-clustered uncertainty, BH families and outcome-blind omission set were retained (Table S1.13; Figure 5c). These analyses adjust cyclic collection timing but do not classify bud, anthesis or post-anthesis stage.

A third contract was frozen before native-range outcomes were inspected (`analysis/ch1/native_range_sensitivity_contract.json`). Source names were resolved to accepted WCVP taxa, and accepted-taxon distribution records were joined to World Geographical Scheme for Recording Plant Distributions level-3 units (Govaerts 2026; Brummitt 2001). The WCVP checklist release, dataset timestamp and licence and the exact TDWG geometry commit and SHA-256 hash were pinned. Coordinates were assigned to level-3 polygons; observations were retained only when the accepted taxon was explicitly native in the mapped unit. Introduced, unlisted, geographically unmapped and ambiguous or unresolved taxon records were excluded rather than inferred. The 36 primary models were refitted on endpoint-specific native-only cohorts with taxon-clustered uncertainty and a separate BH family. A frozen non-circular row was retained only when its sign matched the primary coefficient and its native-only q value was below 0.05 (Table S1.14; Figure 5c).

High-resolution traits were analysed in the ≤10-km complete-case cohort of 904 observations from 165 taxa. The multivariable within-taxon model included all four CHELSA predictors, log minimum head dimension and log1p image sharpness. Climate-row BH correction covered the 12 full-cohort endpoint–predictor tests. BIO4 also had to remain positive within fixed minimum-dimension strata of 150–199, 200–299 and ≥300 pixels to retain a headline claim (Table S1.6).

Source metadata were screened without trait outcomes to identify observations with at least two public photographs for a taxon → observation → photograph → head decomposition (Table S1.12). Image remeasurement and same-individual adjudication remain pending.

## Among-taxon environmental and phylogenetic analyses

For taxa with at least five observations, medians of the nine traits and available environmental predictors were calculated. Complete taxa were projected onto the first three axes of standardized environmental PCA. Environmental separation between lower and upper trait quartiles was summarized by centroid distance and Gaussian Bhattacharyya overlap. Each trait was permuted among the same taxa 10,000 times while environmental coordinates, availability and the trait distribution were fixed. BH correction was applied across nine traits separately for each metric. The analysis was repeated for taxa directly represented in the dated backbone (Table S1.7; Figure S1.8).

Taxon medians from the atlas were related to the four CHELSA predictors by OLS and phylogenetic generalized least squares (PGLS). Brownian and maximum-likelihood Pagel-λ models used the dated `GBOTB.extended.LCVP` backbone. Only 54 of 216 atlas taxa were direct tips. Remaining taxa were placed within genus using alternative `V.PhyloMaker2` scenarios: deterministic scenarios 1 and 3 and 50 randomized scenario-2 trees. An association was considered insensitive to tested placements when direction was stable, the 2.5–97.5% coefficient range excluded zero and nominal support was widespread; we also report the fraction passing within-tree FDR correction. Because retained Pagel-λ estimates were zero and direct-backbone signal was unsupported, this layer is presented only as a Supporting Information placement sensitivity (Table S1.8; Figure S1.9).

## Robustness and interpretation

The 259 source names were audited against the World Checklist of Vascular Plants (WCVP). Eight synonym candidates were collapsed simultaneously, after which nested variance and all 36 primary climate models were recomputed (Table S1.10). For each endpoint, the best-supported SPDE specification was refitted using the same centring, predictors, mesh and priors to obtain residuals. Global Moran's I used eight nearest neighbours on unit-sphere coordinates and 999 permutations. Taxon ranks were also recomputed after omitting each broad geographic region (Table S1.9; Figures S1.4–S1.5).

FDR families remained separate by analysis tier, cohort and model family. Cross-sectional associations are not evidence of plasticity, local adaptation, temporal response, pollinator-mediated selection, evolutionary rate or adaptive radiation.
