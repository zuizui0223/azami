# Methods

## Study design and analytical scales

We used public biodiversity photographs to quantify continuous capitulum traits across *Cirsium* and allied Cardueae while retaining repeated observations within source-assigned taxa. Source names were treated as operational taxonomic units rather than as a resolved genus-wide taxonomy, and authority-backed synonym collapse was evaluated separately. Cohort definitions and inferential roles are listed in Table 1 and Table S1.1; geographic coverage and filtering are shown in Figure 2 and Figures S1.6–S1.7.

Two source streams answered complementary questions. A balanced image atlas contained 3,725 observations, 6,626 detected capitula and 216 taxa. Each observation contributed one photograph, which could contain multiple heads. This layer supported the established nested visible-variance decomposition, taxon-level summaries and historical-placement sensitivities. An exhaustive stream applied detection and measurement before trait-based thinning. It contained 406,582 detector-positive observations from 286 taxa. Coordinate filtering retained 392,989 observations from 271 taxa; a positional-accuracy threshold of ≤10 km retained 297,293 observations from 259 taxa; and one observation per taxon × 0.25° cell yielded the primary cohort of 46,276 observations from 259 taxa. Statistical families from different cohorts were kept separate.

The analyses were organized by biological scale. Within-taxon models used repeated observations after removing taxon means. Among-taxon models used taxon medians. Whole-capitulum analyses used observations complete for all 18 measured inferential endpoints and compared association geometry at the two scales. None of these cross-sectional scales was interpreted as a direct estimate of plasticity, adaptation or evolutionary change.

## Detection and deterministic continuous measurements

Traits were eligible when they were repeatedly visible, numerically definable, explicitly assessable or unassessable and complementary in measurement scope (Table 2; Table S1.2). Fine indumentum, glandularity, mucilage, absolute size without scale and whole-plant characters were excluded rather than coded as absent.

Visible capitula were localized with a single-class You Only Look Once version 11 nano detector initialized from `yolo11n.pt` and trained for 40 epochs at 640-pixel input size on 270 pseudo-labelled images. The confidence threshold was 0.25. Detection defined regions of interest only; downstream traits were calculated by deterministic image functions. Source observation and photograph identifiers, bounding boxes and contextual crops were retained.

Nine established primary endpoints represented three broad measurement modules: orientation angle relative to Exchangeable Image File Format (EXIF)-oriented image vertical; visible corolla colour as Commission Internationale de l'Éclairage L*a*b* lightness, chroma and sine/cosine components of circular hue; and outline aspect ratio, circularity, solidity and width-profile coefficient of variation. The frozen orientation scale was 0 degrees upward, 90 degrees horizontal and 180 degrees downward in the EXIF-oriented image. Image vertical is a reproducible reference, not an inclinometer measurement of gravity. Hue components were interpreted jointly. Failed or unstable measurements remained missing. Horizontal mirroring provided a technical repeatability check. In the balanced atlas, 5,777 heads were usable for colour, 5,324 for outline and 4,585 for orientation.

For GEB v2, we froze a category-free 27-endpoint contract spanning eight measurement modules. The inferential tier comprised the nine established primary endpoints plus ten candidate endpoints for visible floral display and finer involucral/armature geometry. Four colour-composition endpoints were descriptive and four surface-image endpoints were validation-only. Candidate architecture endpoints included involucre length/width ratio, apical and basal taper ratios, radial projection roughness, upper-tail and maximum projection, outward spread fraction, projection-peak density and bilateral asymmetry. These were image-derived continuous phenotypes and were not relabelled as botanical spine length, recurvature, stiffness, hair density, glandularity or secretion.

The final artifact-backed GEB v2 run measured 22 of 27 registered endpoints. Eighteen of 19 inferential endpoints were available: all nine primary endpoints and nine candidates. `visible_floret_fraction` was the only unexecuted inferential endpoint, and four descriptive colour pixel-fraction endpoints were also unexecuted. The resulting trait universe contained 46,276 observations from 259 taxa and 374,255 analysis-eligible observation × endpoint values. Missingness represented failed or unexecuted measurement and was never converted to a categorical state or biological zero.

A detector-audit packet was sampled after excluding every photograph and observation used in detector development. It comprised 1,000 images from 323 taxa and 85 ten-degree spatial blocks; 250 images were assigned to a second annotator. Adjudicated human boxes remain incomplete, so detector precision and recall are not used as biological evidence. Independent reference measurements are likewise required for continuous-trait accuracy; retention rates and mirror repeatability do not provide that validation.

## High-resolution and expanded candidate geometry

The legacy high-resolution contour layer used a minimum detected-head dimension of 150 pixels. After resolution, sharpness, segmentation and mirror checks, 1,443 heads from 1,292 observations and 210 taxa were usable. Three auxiliary endpoints described two-dimensional projection roughness, outward-positive radial-projection fraction and maximum projection relative to head radius. These metrics could not distinguish longer bract tips from spreading or recurved bracts and were not treated as direct defensive measurements.

The GEB v2 expanded layer was selected without trait outcomes. Among 1,255,791 detected heads, 322,517 met the ≥150-pixel rule. Intersecting the frozen 46,276-observation cohort and retaining one largest head per observation yielded 22,186 selected observations from 235 taxa; expanded measurement completed for 22,177 heads. Contour-derived architecture was usable for 2,326 heads and validation-only surface metrics for 909. Climate-complete candidate models used 2,277 observations from 78 taxa.

## Visible variation and taxon morphospace

For each established primary endpoint, total visible image sums of squares were partitioned among source-assigned taxon means, among photographs within taxa and among heads within photographs. Taxa were resampled as clusters 2,000 times for uncertainty intervals. Two sensitivities retained one deterministic head per photograph or sampled 10 photographs per eligible taxon in 500 balanced replicates (Table S1.4; Figure S1.1). The components describe visible image variation, not genetic variance.

Taxon medians for the nine primary endpoints were standardized and analysed by principal components analysis (PCA). GEB v2 added a separate expanded taxon morphospace. Taxon-level summaries were standardized, complete taxa were retained within each registered scope and PCA was fitted to all 18 measured inferential endpoints and to measurement modules separately. This expanded PCA did not replace the frozen taxon → photograph → head decomposition, which remained defined for the nine primary endpoints.

## Within-taxon endpoint–environment associations

Four CHELSA v2.1 predictors represented annual mean temperature (BIO1), temperature seasonality (BIO4), annual precipitation (BIO12) and precipitation seasonality (BIO15) for 1981–2010. In the primary cohort, traits and predictors were demeaned within taxon and standardized. Ordinary least-squares coefficients were fitted without an intercept using taxon-clustered standard errors. Benjamini–Hochberg (BH) false-discovery-rate correction was applied across 36 endpoint-component models (Table S1.3; Figure S1.2).

Each primary endpoint was also fitted with Gaussian stochastic partial differential equation models in integrated nested Laplace approximation. Four predefined predictor groups contained climate, climate plus topography, climate plus soil or all predictors. Models included a taxon random intercept and Matérn spatial field, and each endpoint used the same complete cases across groups. Posterior two-sided tail areas were BH-adjusted globally and within groups; coefficient direction and credible intervals were compared across groups (Table S1.5; Figure S1.3).

The expanded trait-universe screen used the same four predictors and within-taxon standardization. Linear endpoints used ordinary least squares with taxon-clustered standard errors; circular hue was treated as one joint test. Multiplicity correction was performed separately in primary and candidate tiers. Candidate rows were not eligible for botanical or functional promotion from this screen alone.

## Predeclared sampling, range and image-quality controls

The primary bias-control contract residualized every primary endpoint and predictor within taxon on an intercept plus sine and cosine of observation day-of-year, then refitted the 36 models with a separate BH family. Omission taxa were selected only by observation count: each of the ten largest taxa was removed separately, and the two largest taxa were removed jointly. A second frozen contract tested hemisphere-aligned cyclic dates and separate hemisphere-specific curves while retaining the same model family and omission set. These analyses adjust collection timing but do not identify bud, anthesis or post-anthesis stage.

A third contract joined accepted WCVP taxa to TDWG level-3 native distributions. Coordinates were retained only when the accepted taxon was explicitly native in the mapped unit. Introduced, unlisted, unmapped and unresolved records were excluded rather than inferred. The 36 primary models were refitted on endpoint-specific native-only cohorts with a separate BH family. A frozen non-circular row was classified native-range robust only when its sign matched the primary estimate and native-only q < 0.05.

The legacy three-endpoint contour screen was refitted with all four CHELSA predictors, log minimum head dimension and log1p sharpness. Climate-row BH correction covered 12 tests, and BIO4 also had to retain direction in 150–199, 200–299 and ≥300-pixel strata. None passed the predeclared confirmation.

For the nine measurable GEB v2 candidate endpoints, models included the four CHELSA predictors plus log minimum head dimension and log1p sharpness, producing 36 adjusted climate rows. Models were repeated within the same three resolution strata. Directional persistence after adjustment and across successful strata yielded `C_exploratory_quality_robust`; material sign instability yielded `C_exploratory_image_sensitive`. Passing this audit did not convert an image phenotype into a direct botanical measurement.

Source metadata were screened without trait outcomes to identify observations with at least two public photographs for a future taxon → observation → photograph → head decomposition. The frozen cohort contains 20,073 observations from 236 taxa, but trait remeasurement and same-individual adjudication remain pending.

## Matched among-taxon endpoint analyses

The established nine-primary-trait sorting analysis summarized taxa with at least five observations by trait and environment medians. Complete taxa were projected onto three standardized environmental PCA axes. Environmental separation between lower and upper trait quartiles was summarized by centroid distance and Gaussian Bhattacharyya overlap. Each trait was permuted among the same taxa 10,000 times while environmental coordinates, availability and trait distribution were fixed. BH correction was applied separately to each metric, and the analysis was repeated for direct dated-backbone taxa.

To place the expanded trait universe on matched biological scales, we froze `analysis/ch1/within_among_alignment_contract.json`. The 18 measured endpoints formed 17 inferential units because circular hue was joint. For each linear endpoint, taxon medians required at least five usable measurements in the main scope and models required at least 20 taxa. Taxon environments were medians of BIO1, BIO4, BIO12 and BIO15 across the frozen spatial cohort. Trait and predictor were standardized among taxa and fitted as a univariate slope. Two-sided P values came from 10,000 predictor-label permutations among the same taxa. Joint circular hue combined standardized sine and cosine slopes as one magnitude under paired permutation. BH correction was separate for primary and candidate tiers. A minimum-two-measurement scope was frozen as a replication sensitivity. Each endpoint × predictor row was classified as supported at both scales, within only, among only or neither.

A second contract extended environmental-PCA sorting to common taxa complete across the measured inferential universe. The main scope required at least five measurements for every endpoint; a threshold of two was the sensitivity. Lower and upper quartiles of each of 16 linear taxon-median endpoints were compared by centroid distance and Bhattacharyya overlap with 10,000 label permutations. Joint hue was not ordered into quartiles; paired sine/cosine values were regressed jointly on environmental PC1–PC3 and tested by paired permutation.

## Whole-capitulum association geometry

Whole-capitulum analyses began from observations with finite analysis-eligible values for all 18 measured inferential endpoints. Taxa were retained at thresholds of at least five complete observations for the main analysis or at least two for sensitivity. Circular hue sine/cosine were converted analytically into one joint inferential unit, giving 17 units across the registered orientation, colour, shape, involucre-architecture and armature modules.

For the within-taxon matrix, each endpoint was centred by its taxon mean. Correlations used inverse taxon sample-size weights, so each retained taxon contributed equal total weight. For the among-taxon matrix, correlations used taxon medians. Associations between two linear units were absolute correlations. Association between joint hue and another endpoint was the multiple-correlation magnitude obtained from the endpoint's correlations with sine and cosine and the 2 × 2 hue-component correlation matrix.

For each 17 × 17 strength matrix, the module statistic was mean within-module association minus mean between-module association. Significance used 10,000 permutations of module labels while preserving module sizes. Cross-scale correspondence was Spearman correlation between the upper triangles of the within- and among-taxon matrices; its null jointly permuted trait labels on the among-taxon matrix 10,000 times. Taxon-cluster bootstrap resampling with 1,000 replicates provided 95% intervals for both module contrasts and matrix similarity. These statistics tested measurement-module organization, not functional or genetic modularity.

## Process-based environmental blocks

The four established CHELSA predictors remained the core representation. Before inspecting extension outcomes, we froze five additional variables: mean shortwave radiation, mean vapour-pressure deficit, mean near-surface wind, growing-season precipitation and potential NPP. Sources and aggregation rules were pinned in `analysis/ch1/chelsa_process_environment_sources.json`, and every final variable had to meet a ≥98% coverage gate. No unavailable variable could be replaced after inspecting phenotype outcomes. Extraction was restricted to the complete-18 cohort to reduce raster input/output without selecting on trait magnitude.

Six predeclared blocks were evaluated: `core_thermal` (BIO1 + BIO4), `core_precipitation` (BIO12 + BIO15), `radiative_atmospheric_drying` (shortwave radiation + VPD), `mechanical_exposure` (wind), `growing_season_water_input` (growing-season precipitation) and `climatic_productivity` (potential NPP). The response was the full 18-endpoint vector. Within-taxon traits and predictors were taxon centred, weighted by inverse taxon sample size and standardized using those weights. Among-taxon models used standardized taxon medians. Multivariate R² was the weighted or unweighted fitted sum of squares divided by total standardized response sum of squares.

Each block received one permutation test at each scale and replication threshold. Within taxa, complete predictor rows were permuted only within taxon; among taxa, predictor rows were permuted among taxa. Each permutation moved the entire predictor row and preserved the 18-response covariance. BH correction was applied across the six blocks separately within each scale and replication scope. Standardized endpoint coefficient matrices and module-wise coefficient-energy allocations were exported descriptively. Within/among coefficient-matrix cosine similarity was accompanied by a 1,000-replicate taxon-cluster bootstrap and was not used as a causal or selection statistic.

## Canonical full-27 / full-environment endpoint atlas

To answer the Chapter 1 question without importing the old nine-endpoint selection, we versioned a separate retrospective exploratory lane in `analysis/ch1/v2_full27_environment_atlas_contract.json`. All 27 registered endpoints entered an inventory. Every endpoint with a finite `measurement_available` value was analysed regardless of its historical tier, while unexecuted endpoints remained explicit status rows. Hue sine and cosine were retained as two registered measurements but formed one joint circular inferential unit. The whole-capitulum complete-case cohort was not used to select endpoint rows.

For each measured endpoint, observation-level sum of squares was partitioned into variation below and between source-assigned taxon means. A 500-replicate equal-replication sensitivity sampled two observations without replacement from every taxon having at least two measurements. These partitions quantify visible image-phenotype information compressed by a taxon mean; they are not estimates of heritability, plastic variance or within-individual change. Geographic support was reported as observation and taxon counts, five-degree occupied cells, coordinate bounds and observed value quantiles.

The four core CHELSA variables and the five process variables were extracted for the full phenotype-blind 46,276-observation cohort before endpoint-specific missingness filtering. The six blocks were thermal (BIO1, BIO4), hydric (BIO12, BIO15), radiative/atmospheric (shortwave radiation, VPD), mechanical (wind), growing-season water input (GSP) and resource/productivity (potential NPP). GSP was a climatic growing-season layer rather than taxon-specific flowering-season rainfall, and NPP was modelled potential productivity rather than measured local resources. Each variable had to retain at least 98% coverage.

Within-taxon linear models used taxon-demeaned standardized traits and predictors, no intercept and taxon-clustered standard errors; eligibility required at least 100 observations, ten taxa and two observations per retained taxon. Joint hue used the magnitude of its standardized sine and cosine slopes and 9,999 predictor permutations restricted within taxon. Among-taxon models paired taxon trait medians with taxon environmental medians, required at least 20 taxa and used 9,999 predictor-label permutations. The primary scope required five trait observations per taxon, with two as a separately corrected sensitivity. BH correction formed one family across every successful unit x nine-predictor row separately for within taxa, among min5 and among min2. Historical analysis tiers neither split multiplicity nor removed rows.

Because the public-image cohort was strongly unbalanced by taxon and region, a separate retrospective sampling-composition contract was frozen after the full-27 atlas had been inspected. It admitted the 26 within-taxon and ten primary among-taxon global-q-supported rows without removing any other atlas row. Taxa were ranked using environment-cohort observation counts only. Each of the ten most observed taxa was omitted separately and the two largest were omitted jointly. Within-taxon rows were also refitted with observation weights of 1/n within taxon, giving every retained taxon equal total weight. Each of six Natural Earth broad regions was omitted separately, with trait and environmental summaries recomputed after omission, and a native-only scope retained only observations explicitly classified native by the frozen WCVP/TDWG lookup. Linear stability required sign preservation; joint hue required positive cosine alignment of the sine-cosine coefficient vectors, equivalent to an angular difference below 90 degrees. These selected-row checks exported coefficients and effect-magnitude ratios only. They generated no post-selection P values, no new multiplicity family and no correction to a probability sample.

Every primary min5 endpoint-gradient row was then classified as supported at both scales, within only, among only, neither or not comparable. Only q < 0.05 rows entered a new v2-native broad-space screen. We added a second-order basis of unit-sphere coordinates and used Freedman-Lane residual permutations; within-taxon permutations remained restricted within source-assigned taxa. Passage required permutation P < 0.05, preservation of sign for linear endpoints and residual eight-nearest-neighbour Moran P >= 0.05 on a deterministic maximum of 5,000 observations. This broad-trend screen is not equivalent to the older SPDE analysis and cannot remove every spatial process.

Among-taxon pairs passing the broad-space screen entered maximum-likelihood Pagel-lambda PGLS on all 52 audited placement trees: deterministic scenarios 1 and 3 and 50 randomized scenario-2 trees. Passage required a successful fit, P < 0.05 and stable linear direction on every tree. A pair passing both sequential screens was labelled an `adaptive_pattern_candidate_under_current_controls`. This label denotes stability under the tested broad-space and placement uncertainties, not a simultaneous spatial-phylogenetic causal model or evidence of heritability, selection, fitness benefit, mechanism, independent origin or convergence.

## Nested core-four sufficiency test

A separately frozen nested analysis tested whether process variables added information beyond BIO1, BIO4, BIO12 and BIO15. The reduced model always contained the four core predictors. One omnibus full model added all five process variables. Four block-specific full models added radiation + VPD, wind, growing-season precipitation or potential NPP. The omnibus was one predeclared test per scale and replication scope and was not included in the block-specific BH family; BH correction covered the four block-specific comparisons separately within each scale and scope.

For each comparison we reported core R², full R², delta R² and partial R² = delta R² / (1 − core R²). Inference used a Freedman–Lane reduced-model residual permutation. Within taxa, the complete 18-response residual vector for one observation could move only among observations of the same taxon; among taxa, residual vectors were permuted among taxa. Whole response rows were permuted intact, preserving covariance among endpoints under the null. The core was deemed adequate only when the main-scope omnibus was unsupported and no stable block-specific increment survived the frozen multiplicity and replication interpretation.

## Spatial, taxonomic and historical robustness

The 259 source names were audited against WCVP. Eight synonym candidates were collapsed simultaneously, after which nested variance and all 36 primary models were recomputed. For each primary endpoint, the best-supported SPDE specification was refitted to export residuals. Global Moran's I used eight nearest neighbours on unit-sphere coordinates and 999 permutations. Taxon ranks were also recomputed after omitting each broad geographic region.

Taxon medians were related to the four CHELSA predictors by ordinary least squares and phylogenetic generalized least squares. Brownian and maximum-likelihood Pagel-λ models used the dated `GBOTB.extended.LCVP` backbone. Only 54 of 216 atlas taxa were direct tips. Remaining taxa were placed within genus under deterministic scenarios 1 and 3 and 50 randomized scenario-2 trees. Because most taxa required placement and retained Pagel-λ estimates were zero, this layer was treated as Supporting Information sensitivity rather than resolved phylogenetic correction.

## Evidence grading and chapter boundary

Earlier frozen result families retained their cohort-specific A/B/C provenance. The canonical full-27 / full-environment lane did not use those grades for entry, multiplicity or row suppression: every registered endpoint and every endpoint-predictor status remained visible. The retrospective sampling-composition audit annotated fragility without changing candidate labels, while only the declared q, spatial and historical gates changed the final candidate label. Whole-capitulum organization, stand-alone environmental blocks, nested incremental tests and coefficient geometry remained secondary result layers and did not promote or demote full-27 endpoint rows.

Cross-sectional associations were not evidence of plasticity, local adaptation, pollinator-mediated selection, antagonist defence, evolutionary rate or adaptive radiation. Azami ended after freezing continuous phenotype distributions, multilevel geometry and environmental organization. Downstream generative-history tests may ask which mechanism families reproduce those targets, but cannot convert them into causal observations.
