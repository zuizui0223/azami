# Main figure captions

**Figure 1. From public photographs to continuous multiscale thistle phenomics.** Open-licensed photographs from the global *Cirsium* and allied Cardueae atlas illustrate the measurement route. An object detector localizes visible capitula; examples show established orientation, visible-colour and outline measurements together with finer continuous involucral and armature geometry from the expanded GEB v2 contract. Repeated image-derived phenotypes support nested visible-variance analysis, taxon morphospace, within-taxon environmental associations, matched among-taxon comparisons and whole-capitulum organization. The examples illustrate workflow and measurement scope and are not independent accuracy tests.

**Figure 2. Geographic sampling and analytical domain of global *Cirsium* and allied Cardueae photographs.** (a) Density per 1° cell among detector-positive observations with finite coordinates. (b) One-degree display-cell density for 46,276 observations from 259 taxa in the primary taxon × 0.25° spatially thinned cohort. (c) Complementary balanced-atlas and exhaustive/spatially thinned data streams, including the complete-18 cohort used for multilevel phenotype-space analysis. (d) Occupied environmental domain under CHELSA 1981–2010 normals. Maps use a Mollweide equal-area projection and diagnose sampling of public photographs, not global representativeness of all thistles.

**Figure 3. Nested decomposition of visible capitulum-trait variance in the global thistle atlas.** For each of nine established primary continuous endpoints, visible image sums of squares are partitioned among source-assigned taxon means, among photographs within taxa and among detected heads within photographs. The combined below-taxon fraction ranges from 0.589 to 0.931. Components describe visible image phenotypes, not genetic variance; Figure S1.1 presents one-head-per-photograph and equal-replication sensitivities.

**Figure 4. Expanded taxon-level capitulum morphospace is multidimensional.** Scores of the 127 taxa complete for all 18 measured inferential continuous endpoints are shown on the first principal-component axes together with standardized endpoint loadings. PC1, PC2 and PC3 explain 18.49%, 12.01% and 11.78% of variance, respectively (42.28% cumulative). Module summaries distinguish orientation, visible colour, gross shape, involucral architecture and armature rather than assigning one categorical morphology. The frozen nine-primary-endpoint PCA is retained in Supporting Information. No PC axis is interpreted as a single biological syndrome.

**Figure 5. The whole-capitulum phenotype is partially organized and its geometry changes across biological scales.** (a) Within-taxon 17-unit association matrix derived from taxon-centred observations with equal total taxon weight. (b) Among-taxon association matrix derived from taxon medians. Registered measurement-module boundaries are shown without implying validated functional or genetic modules. (c) Within-module minus between-module association contrast is 0.164502 within taxa and 0.088475 among taxa, with taxon-bootstrap intervals. (d) The upper triangles of the within- and among-taxon matrices have Spearman similarity 0.366299. The result supports partial phenotypic / measurement-module organization and incomplete cross-scale correspondence.

**Figure 6. Environmental organization is endpoint specific, scale specific and dependent on the environmental representation.** (a) Endpoint-level evidence atlas showing established A/B-grade primary associations and the three registry-wide C-grade candidate associations, with circular hue treated jointly and quality/range sensitivities annotated. (b) Matched classification of 68 endpoint × BIO1/BIO4/BIO12/BIO15 rows as `both_scales`, `within_only`, `among_only` or `neither`; orientation aligns with BIO1 within taxa and BIO12 among taxa, and all three final candidate rows are within only. (c) Nested whole-capitulum comparison of the four-variable core with the five-variable process extension. The extension is unsupported within taxa (partial R² = 0.013535, P = 0.24468) but supported among taxa (partial R² = 0.214978, P = 0.000700); growing-season precipitation is the only block-specific increment supported at both replication thresholds. Evidence grades and nested tests describe cross-sectional spatial structure, not plasticity, adaptation or direct environmental causation.
