# Main figure captions

These captions correspond to the submission-facing six-figure sequence fixed in `FIGURE_TABLE_MAP.md`. Figure assembly is descriptive/presentational only and does not create new inferential families.

**Figure 1. From public photographs to continuous multiscale thistle phenomics.** Open-licensed photographs from the executed balanced atlas illustrate the production measurement route. YOLO is used only to localize visible capitula; a detailed example then shows the tight crop, contextual crop, corolla-colour mask, outline/convex-hull extraction and image-vertical orientation measurement, followed by the resulting continuous numerical values. The added analysis strip shows how repeated head-level phenotypes feed distinct executed questions: nested taxon–photograph–head visible variance, within-taxon environmental analyses, and among-taxon niche/historical analyses. The example demonstrates the production pipeline but does not substitute for the pending independent detector and measurement-validity audits.

**Figure 2. Geographic sampling and analytical domain.** (A) Global 1° density of detector-positive observations before spatial filtering. The frozen detector-positive cohort contains 406,582 observations from 286 taxa; 406,178 records have finite coordinates that can be rendered geographically. (B) Locations of the 46,276 observations from 259 taxa in the primary taxon × 0.25° spatially thinned cohort. (C) The two executed analysis streams: a balanced image atlas used for nested variance, taxon-level PCA and historical summaries, including its high-resolution involucre subset; and an exhaustive detector-positive stream filtered through coordinate availability and ≤10 km positional accuracy to the primary within-taxon and grouped SPDE analyses. (D) CHELSA annual mean temperature (BIO1) versus annual precipitation (BIO12) occupied by the primary cohort. Maps diagnose geographic sampling rather than global representativeness of all *Cirsium*.

**Figure 3. Nested decomposition of visible capitulum-trait variance.** For each of the nine primary continuous endpoints, total visible image sums of squares are partitioned among source-assigned taxon means, among photographs within taxa and among detected heads within photographs. The combined below-taxon fraction ranges from 0.589 to 0.931. These are visible image-phenotype components and are not genetic variance estimates; Figure S1 shows the one-head-per-photo and equal-replication sensitivities.

**Figure 4. Taxon-level capitulum trait architecture is multidimensional.** (A) Scores of the 148 taxa complete for all nine primary endpoints on PC1 and PC2, with arrows showing the directions of all nine standardized trait loadings. PC1 explains 32.9% and PC2 23.2% of taxon-level variance. The main panel is a central zoom so that the dense taxon distribution and loading directions remain legible; the inset retains the full PC1–PC2 range, including the extreme score of *Cirsium kawakamii* (PC1 = −15.6). (B) PC3 loadings, exposing a third independent dimension dominated by aspect ratio and orientation; PC3 explains 13.2%, bringing cumulative PC1–PC3 variance to 69.3%. No PCA axis is interpreted as a single biological syndrome.

**Figure 5. Trait-specific environmental effect sizes across the executed within-taxon analyses.** (A) The eight of 36 primary endpoint-component × CHELSA coefficients that passed BH correction in the exhaustive spatially thinned within-taxon analysis, shown as standardized coefficients with 95% confidence intervals. Hue sine and cosine are computational components of one circular response and require joint interpretation. (B) The eight stable trait × predictor patterns retained across grouped SPDE-INLA model comparisons, showing posterior coefficients and 95% credible intervals for every model group in which each predictor occurs; filled markers identify model-group coefficients with global posterior-tail BH q < 0.05, while open markers retain stability context. (C) The three of 12 high-resolution involucral contour coefficients that passed BH correction, all positive with temperature seasonality. Coefficient scales belong to their respective analysis families and are not pooled numerically across panels.

**Figure 6. Within-taxon and among-taxon environmental structure are not interchangeable.** (A) Supported primary within-taxon (`W`) and randomized-tree among-taxon historical-sensitivity (`H`) climate associations are placed in one endpoint × CHELSA matrix, with `+`/`−` showing coefficient direction. The panel makes agreement, scale-specific support and direction reversals visible without treating the two analyses as one inferential family. (B) Standardized coefficients for the six taxon-level climate associations that remained BH-supported in all 50 randomized Pagel-λ PGLS trees; horizontal intervals show the 2.5–97.5% range across trees. This remains a historical-placement sensitivity because only 54 of 216 atlas taxa are direct dated-backbone tips.
