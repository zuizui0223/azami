# Main figure captions

**Figure 1. From public photographs to continuous multiscale thistle phenomics.** Open-licensed photographs from the global *Cirsium* and allied Cardueae atlas illustrate the measurement route. An object detector localizes visible capitula; detailed examples show established orientation, visible-colour and outline measurements together with finer continuous involucral/armature geometry from the expanded GEB v2 contract. Repeated image-derived phenotypes support nested visible-variance analysis, taxon morphospace, within-taxon environmental associations and among-taxon environmental comparisons. The examples illustrate the workflow and measurement scope and are not independent accuracy tests.

**Figure 2. Geographic sampling and analytical domain of global *Cirsium* and allied Cardueae photographs.** (a) Density per 1° cell among detector-positive observations with finite coordinates. (b) One-degree display-cell density for 46,276 observations from 259 taxa in the primary taxon × 0.25° spatially thinned cohort. (c) Complementary balanced-atlas and exhaustive/spatially thinned data streams, with the trait-blind high-resolution intersection used for expanded candidate geometry. (d) Annual mean temperature (BIO1) and annual precipitation (BIO12) occupied by the primary cohort, using CHELSA 1981–2010 climate normals. Maps use a Mollweide equal-area projection and diagnose sampling of public photographs, not global representativeness of all thistles.

**Figure 3. Nested decomposition of visible capitulum-trait variance in the global thistle atlas.** For each of nine established primary continuous endpoints, visible image sums of squares are partitioned among source-assigned taxon means, among photographs within taxa and among detected heads within photographs. The combined below-taxon fraction ranges from 0.589 to 0.931. Components describe visible image phenotypes, not genetic variance; Figure S1.1 presents one-head-per-photograph and equal-replication sensitivities.

**Figure 4. Expanded taxon-level capitulum morphospace is multidimensional.** Scores of the 127 taxa complete for all 18 measured inferential continuous endpoints are shown on the first principal-component axes together with standardized endpoint loadings. PC1, PC2 and PC3 explain 18.5%, 12.0% and 11.8% of variance, respectively (42.3% cumulative). Module summaries distinguish orientation, visible colour, gross shape, involucral architecture and armature rather than assigning one categorical morphology. The frozen nine-primary-endpoint PCA is retained as a comparison in Supporting Information. No PC axis is interpreted as a single biological syndrome.

**Figure 5. Environmental structure across continuous capitulum traits is evidence graded.** (a) Established within-taxon primary associations, with orientation–BIO1 and aspect ratio–BIO4 marked A-grade robust and same-signed but native-range-sensitive rows marked B-grade. Circular hue components are displayed jointly and remain colour-calibration dependent. (b) Grouped SPDE-INLA context for established endpoints, emphasizing the concentration of spatially modelled support in orientation and visible colour. (c) Expanded candidate associations from the registry-wide screen: involucre length/width ratio–BIO15 and apical taper–BIO12 are `C_exploratory_quality_robust`, whereas `bract_projection_p95`–BIO15 is `C_exploratory_image_sensitive` because of resolution-stratum sign instability. Intervals are 95% confidence intervals where available. Evidence grades describe robustness of cross-sectional image phenotype–environment structure; they do not imply plasticity, adaptation, botanical function or causal mechanism.
