# Main figure captions

**Figure 1. From public photographs to continuous multiscale thistle phenomics.** Open-licensed photographs from the global *Cirsium* and allied Cardueae atlas illustrate the measurement route. An object detector localizes visible capitula; a detailed example shows the crop, corolla-colour mask, outline and convex hull, and orientation relative to image vertical, followed by continuous numerical values. Repeated head-level phenotypes support three analyses: visible variance among taxa, photographs and heads; within-taxon environmental associations; and among-taxon environmental and historical comparisons. The example illustrates the workflow and is not an independent accuracy test.

**Figure 2. Geographic sampling and analytical domain of global *Cirsium* and allied Cardueae photographs.** (a) Density per 1° cell among 406,178 detector-positive observations with finite coordinates. (b) One-degree display-cell density for 46,276 observations from 259 taxa in the primary taxon × 0.25° spatially thinned cohort. (c) Complementary balanced-atlas and exhaustive data streams. (d) Annual mean temperature (BIO1) and annual precipitation (BIO12) occupied by the primary cohort, using CHELSA 1981–2010 climate normals. Maps use a Mollweide equal-area projection and include a 5,000-km equatorial scale bar. They diagnose sampling of public photographs, not global representativeness of all thistles.

**Figure 3. Nested decomposition of visible capitulum-trait variance in the global thistle atlas.** For each of nine continuous endpoints, visible image sums of squares are partitioned among source-assigned taxon means, among photographs within taxa and among detected heads within photographs. The combined below-taxon fraction ranges from 0.589 to 0.931. Components describe visible image phenotypes, not genetic variance; Figure S1.1 presents one-head-per-photograph and equal-replication sensitivities.

**Figure 4. Taxon-level capitulum trait architecture in the global thistle atlas.** (a) Scores of 148 taxa complete for all nine primary endpoints on principal components (PCs) 1 and 2, with standardized trait loadings. PC1 explains 32.9% and PC2 23.2% of taxon-level variance; the inset retains the full range, including *Cirsium kawakamii* (PC1 = −15.6). (b) PC3 loadings; PC3 explains 13.2%, bringing cumulative PC1–PC3 variance to 69.3%. No PC axis is interpreted as a single biological syndrome.

**Figure 5. Trait-specific environmental effect sizes and predeclared bias controls for global thistle photographs.** (a) Eight of 36 endpoint-component × CHELSA coefficients passing Benjamini–Hochberg (BH) correction in the frozen primary analysis, shown with 95% confidence intervals. Hue sine and cosine are components of one circular response. (b) Stable patterns from grouped stochastic partial differential equation models fitted by integrated nested Laplace approximation (SPDE-INLA); filled markers indicate global posterior-tail BH q < 0.05 and open markers provide model-group context. (c) Native-only coefficients for the four non-circular primary rows after all four had retained sign and BH support under the raw-calendar and hemisphere-aware cyclic-season audits and retained sign across predeclared dominant-taxon omissions. Filled markers identify the two rows meeting the frozen native-only same-sign plus BH rule; open markers identify same-sign rows that failed native-only BH correction. Intervals are 95% confidence intervals. Native-range restriction is not developmental-stage control, evidence of adaptation or a reconstruction of introduction history.
