# Conclusion

Global public photographs preserved repeated continuous capitulum measurements at a scale sufficient for within-taxon analysis. At least 58.9% of visible image variance occurred below taxon means across all primary endpoints, while taxon means retained multidimensional differences. All four non-circular primary climate rows survived cyclic collection-timing and dominant-taxon controls, but only orientation–annual mean temperature and aspect ratio–temperature seasonality met the separately frozen native-only sign-plus-BH rule. Effects were small, and the other two climate rows and all three high-resolution involucral rows were withdrawn from the current headline after their respective controls.

These findings show how public-image phenomics can complement taxon-mean databases while preserving uncertainty and failed controls. They do not yet establish the biological share of below-taxon variance or corolla-specific colour gradients: developmental stage, paired colour negative controls, repeat-photo variance and independent detector/trait validation remain open. The native-only restriction does not demonstrate niche response, adaptation or introduction history. All associations are observational, and the historical layer is a Supporting Information placement sensitivity rather than resolved phylogenetic correction.
