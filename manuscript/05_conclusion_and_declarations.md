# Conclusion

Global public photographs can be used to quantify repeated continuous capitulum phenotypes at a scale large enough to describe thistle morphological diversity across geography. Across all primary endpoints, at least 58.9% of visible image variance occurred below taxon means, while taxon means retained multidimensional differences. This shows that species-level summaries and coarse morphological categories capture only part of the phenotype visible in public imagery.

Environmental structure was trait- and scale-specific rather than reducible to one climate-associated thistle morphology. The frozen global within-taxon analysis detected associations involving orientation, visible colour and outline aspect ratio; grouped spatial models concentrated support in orientation and colour, and among-taxon environmental separation was likewise strongest for orientation and visible colour. Orientation–annual mean temperature and aspect ratio–temperature seasonality showed the strongest robustness across the current sensitivity analyses. Chroma–precipitation and aspect-ratio–precipitation retained their global directions but were more sensitive to native-range restriction. High-resolution involucral contour metrics yielded provisional seasonality-associated signals whose strength depended on image resolution and sharpness.

The main biological contribution is therefore a quantitative description of spatial phenotype diversity and its environmental structure, not the identification of one causal mechanism. Public-image phenomics can complement conventional trait databases by replacing single taxon means and coarse state assignments with repeated numerical phenotype distributions and by showing which components of a reproductive structure vary within taxa, among taxa and along environmental gradients.

The remaining limitations define the interpretation of these patterns. Visible image variance still combines biological and photographic variation; developmental stage, paired colour controls and repeat-photo variance remain incompletely resolved; and detector and continuous-trait measurements require independent validation. Native-only restriction is treated as a restricted-domain sensitivity rather than a universal replacement for the global estimand. None of the reported associations by itself demonstrates phenotypic plasticity, local adaptation, selection, pollinator causation, defence or evolutionary rate. Instead, the global patterns identify where mechanistic field and evolutionary tests should be targeted next.
