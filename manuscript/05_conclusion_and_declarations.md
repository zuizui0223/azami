# Conclusion

Global public photographs can be used to quantify repeated continuous capitulum phenotypes at a scale large enough to describe thistle morphological diversity across geography. The analysis began with all 27 GEB v2 endpoints: 22 were measured and five remained explicit unexecuted rows. Across the measured endpoints, 81.4-98.6% of raw observation-level variation lay below source-assigned taxon means; after equalizing replication to two observations per eligible taxon, the endpoint medians remained 28.7-58.5%. The 127-taxon complete morphospace also required several axes, with PC1 explaining only 18.5% and the first three PCs 42.3%. Taxon-level summaries and coarse morphological categories therefore capture only part of a genuinely multidimensional visible phenotype landscape.

Environmental structure was trait- and scale-specific rather than reducible to one climate-associated thistle morphology. Across the canonical 234-row full-environment grid, seven endpoint-gradient pairs were supported at both scales, 18 within taxa only, three among taxa only and 152 at neither scale; 54 were not comparable. Most supported involucral and surface-proxy associations occurred within taxa only, while orientation aligned with annual mean temperature within taxa and annual precipitation among taxa. Joint hue aligned with several gradients at both raw scales but retained residual spatial autocorrelation, showing why geographic organization and adaptive candidacy must remain separate.

The process-extension analysis clarifies what the original four climate variables do and do not capture. BIO1, BIO4, BIO12 and BIO15 were adequate for the current within-taxon 18D whole-capitulum estimand: adding radiation, vapour-pressure deficit, wind, growing-season precipitation and potential NPP produced no supported incremental multivariate signal. Among taxa, however, the same five variables added substantial information beyond the four-variable core at both replication thresholds. Growing-season precipitation was the only block-specific increment supported in both scopes; radiation plus atmospheric drying and potential productivity were supported only in the stricter main scope. The four variables are therefore a useful frozen baseline, not a complete representation of among-taxon environmental structure.

The whole-capitulum analysis is a secondary synthesis of these decomposed patterns. In the main complete-18 scope, association among endpoints assigned to the same registered measurement module exceeded association between modules both within and among taxa, while the within- and among-taxon 17-unit association matrices were only partly aligned. The first three morphospace axes explained only 42.3%. The capitulum is therefore multidimensional and partially organized, but the synthesis does not replace trait-specific geography or establish functional or genetic modules.

The main biological contribution is a quantitative description of present continuous-trait geography, not the identification of one causal mechanism. The public-image sample is geographically extensive but uneven, and the new sampling-composition audit retains direction for 16/26 selected within-taxon and 10/10 selected among-taxon pairs. Five within-taxon pairs passed the current broad-space screen: orientation-BIO1, chroma-VPD, involucre length/width-BIO15, projection roughness-radiation and surface high-frequency energy-BIO1. Only four also retained direction throughout the sampling audit; projection roughness-radiation reversed under equal taxon weighting and joint omission of the two most observed taxa. Two among-taxon pairs—lower chroma with higher shortwave radiation and a larger signed head-axis angle relative to EXIF-image vertical with higher annual precipitation—retained direction across all sampling perturbations and support across all 52 historical-placement trees. They are adaptive-pattern candidates under current sequential controls, not demonstrations of adaptation or globally representative effects. Radiation-responsive anthocyanin production and rain shielding are biologically grounded hypotheses for these two rows, but visible CIELAB chroma does not measure pigment concentration and image vertical does not measure gravity; neither mediator is demonstrated. The frozen handoff supports an ordered EAzami programme: I, validate candidate functions; II, reconstruct repeated history on nuclear ancestry; III, distinguish independent origins from retention, ancestral polymorphism and introgression; and IV, test functional or adaptive convergence under the required selection and fitness evidence.

The remaining limitations define the interpretation of these patterns. Visible image variance still combines biological and photographic variation; developmental stage, paired colour controls and repeat-photo variance remain incompletely resolved; and detector and continuous-trait measurements require independent validation. `visible_floret_fraction` was not executed in the final GEB v2 run, so no inference is made from that endpoint. Native-only restriction is treated as a restricted-domain sensitivity rather than a universal replacement for the global estimand. Neither partial module organization nor the growing-season precipitation increment demonstrates functional or genetic modularity, phenotypic plasticity, local adaptation, selection, pollinator causation, defence, direct rain or water-balance effects, or evolutionary rate. Instead, the evidence-graded global phenotype map identifies where mechanistic field, botanical-validation, phylogenetic and EAzami model-sufficiency tests should be targeted next.
