# Title and abstract

## Preferred title

**Global citizen-science images reveal continuous within-taxon variation and environmental structure in thistle capitulum traits**

## Abstract

### Aim

Broad-scale plant trait datasets often summarize taxa by species means or coarse categorical states. We asked whether public photographs can instead recover repeated continuous capitulum phenotypes, reveal spatial diversity hidden by taxon-level summaries and resolve trait-specific environmental structure when reproductive morphology is represented by an expanded quantitative trait set.

### Location

Global.

### Time period

Observations dated 1985–2026; climate normals for 1981–2010.

### Major taxa studied

Source-assigned taxa of *Cirsium* and allied Cardueae.

### Methods

An object detector localized capitula and deterministic image functions measured a frozen 27-endpoint continuous-trait contract spanning established orientation, visible-colour and outline traits plus finer floral, involucral and armature geometry. The final trait universe contained 46,276 spatially thinned observations from 259 taxa; 22 endpoints were measured, including 18 of 19 inferential endpoints (9 primary and 9 candidate). We quantified below-taxon visible variation, taxon morphospace and within-taxon climate associations, retained grouped spatial and among-taxon analyses for established traits, and used endpoint-specific resolution and sharpness sensitivities to grade expanded candidate signals. Phylogenetic analyses remained Supporting Information placement sensitivities.

### Results

Across the nine established primary endpoints, 0.589–0.931 of visible image variance occurred below taxon means, with one-head-per-photograph and equal-replication sensitivities retaining the pattern. The expanded all-inferential morphospace contained 127 complete taxa × 18 endpoints; PC1, PC2 and PC3 explained 18.5%, 12.0% and 11.8%, respectively, indicating multidimensional rather than single-axis capitulum diversity. In the frozen primary analysis, orientation increased with annual mean temperature and outline aspect ratio increased with temperature seasonality; both were robust across the relevant sensitivity layers. Chroma–annual-precipitation and aspect-ratio–annual-precipitation associations retained direction but were range-sensitive, while grouped spatial and among-taxon analyses concentrated support in orientation and visible colour. The expanded candidate screen identified involucre length/width ratio increasing with precipitation seasonality (β = 0.0549, q = 0.000282) and apical taper decreasing with annual precipitation (β = −0.0497, q = 0.000821); both retained direction after image-quality adjustment and across fixed resolution strata, but remain exploratory pending botanical calibration. A bract-projection signal was resolution-sensitive.

### Main conclusions

Global public photographs can move broad-scale trait ecology beyond species means and coarse categories by preserving repeated quantitative phenotype distributions and resolving multiple dimensions of one reproductive structure. Thistle capitulum diversity shows trait- and scale-specific environmental structure rather than one universal climate-associated morphology. The expanded continuous layer reveals additional quality-robust candidate geometry without converting image proxies into botanical or causal claims. The study therefore provides an evidence-graded global map of phenotypic pattern and environment–trait structure; it does not establish phenotypic plasticity, adaptation or causal mechanisms, and independent detector and trait validation remain necessary.
