# Title and abstract

## Preferred title

**Global citizen-science images reveal continuous and scale-dependent environmental structure in thistle capitulum traits**

## Abstract

### Aim

Broad-scale plant trait datasets often summarize taxa by species means or coarse categorical states. We asked whether public photographs can instead recover repeated continuous capitulum phenotypes, reveal spatial diversity hidden by taxon-level summaries and resolve how phenotype–environment structure differs within versus among taxa when reproductive morphology is represented by an expanded quantitative trait set.

### Location

Global.

### Time period

Observations dated 1985–2026; climate normals for 1981–2010.

### Major taxa studied

Source-assigned taxa of *Cirsium* and allied Cardueae.

### Methods

An object detector localized capitula and deterministic image functions measured a frozen 27-endpoint continuous-trait contract spanning established orientation, visible-colour and outline traits plus finer floral, involucral and armature geometry. The final trait universe contained 46,276 spatially thinned observations from 259 taxa; 22 endpoints were measured, including 18 of 19 inferential endpoints (9 primary and 9 candidate). We quantified below-taxon visible variation and taxon morphospace, fitted within-taxon associations with four CHELSA predictors, and then used the same measured trait universe to test taxon-median associations and environmental sorting among taxa. Among-taxon inference used predeclared replication thresholds, 10,000 taxon-label permutations and separate multiplicity families for primary and candidate traits. Endpoint-specific resolution and sharpness sensitivities graded expanded candidate signals; phylogenetic analyses remained Supporting Information placement sensitivities.

### Results

Across the nine established primary endpoints, 0.589–0.931 of visible image variance occurred below taxon means, with one-head-per-photograph and equal-replication sensitivities retaining the pattern. The expanded all-inferential morphospace contained 127 complete taxa × 18 endpoints; PC1, PC2 and PC3 explained 18.5%, 12.0% and 11.8%, respectively. Within taxa, orientation increased with annual mean temperature and outline aspect ratio increased with temperature seasonality; both were robust across the relevant sensitivity layers. The expanded candidate screen identified involucre length/width ratio increasing with precipitation seasonality (β = 0.0549, q = 0.000282) and apical taper decreasing with annual precipitation (β = −0.0497, q = 0.000821); both retained direction after image-quality adjustment and across fixed resolution strata, whereas a bract-projection signal was resolution-sensitive. In the matched among-taxon analysis, none of the nine candidate endpoints passed FDR. Orientation instead showed an among-taxon association with annual precipitation, while joint circular hue contained the clearest partial cross-scale support. Across 68 matched endpoint–climate rows, only three were FDR-supported at both scales, eight were within-taxon only and one was among-taxon only.

### Main conclusions

Global public photographs can move broad-scale trait ecology beyond species means and coarse categories by preserving repeated quantitative phenotype distributions and resolving multiple dimensions of one reproductive structure. Thistle capitulum phenotype–environment structure is not only trait-specific but scale-specific: fine involucral signals detected within taxa do not translate into detectable climatic sorting among taxa, and even orientation aligns with different climatic axes within and among taxa. Public-image phenomics therefore provides an evidence-graded map of continuous phenotypic geography across biological scales rather than one universal climate-associated morphology. The study does not establish phenotypic plasticity, adaptation or causal mechanisms, and independent detector, colour and botanical trait validation remain necessary.
