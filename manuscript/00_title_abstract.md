# Title and abstract

## Preferred title

**Global citizen-science images reveal continuous within-taxon variation and environmental structure in thistle capitulum traits**

## Abstract

### Aim

Broad-scale plant trait datasets often summarize taxa by species means or coarse categorical states. We asked whether public photographs can instead recover repeated continuous capitulum phenotypes, reveal the spatial diversity hidden by species-level summaries, and identify how different components of thistle reproductive morphology are structured along environmental gradients.

### Location

Global.

### Time period

Observations dated 1985–2026; climate normals for 1981–2010.

### Major taxa studied

Source-assigned taxa of *Cirsium* and allied Cardueae.

### Methods

An object detector localized capitula; deterministic functions measured nine continuous orientation, visible-colour and outline endpoints. A balanced atlas comprised 6,626 heads from 3,725 observations and 216 taxa; a spatially thinned cohort comprised 46,276 observations from 259 taxa. We partitioned visible variation among taxa, photographs and heads; fitted within-taxon climate and grouped spatial models; evaluated collection timing, dominant-taxon and native-range sensitivities; and compared among-taxon environmental separation with 10,000 trait-label permutations. Phylogenetic analyses were retained as Supporting Information sensitivity only.

### Results

Across endpoints, 0.589–0.931 of visible image variance occurred below taxon means, and one-head-per-photograph and equal-replication sensitivities retained this pattern. In the frozen global within-taxon analysis, significant environmental structure involved orientation, visible colour and outline aspect ratio, including temperature, precipitation and seasonality gradients; circular hue was interpreted jointly. All four non-circular primary associations retained direction and Benjamini–Hochberg support after cyclic collection-timing adjustment and retained direction across predeclared dominant-taxon omissions. Native-range restriction retained the strongest support for orientation–annual mean temperature and aspect ratio–temperature seasonality, while the chroma–precipitation and aspect-ratio–precipitation associations remained same-signed but lost multiplicity support. Grouped spatial models concentrated support in orientation and visible colour, and among-taxon environmental separation was likewise strongest for orientation and colour. Preliminary involucral contour signals were sensitive to image resolution and sharpness.

### Main conclusions

Global public photographs can move broad-scale trait ecology beyond species means and coarse categories by preserving repeated quantitative phenotype distributions. Thistle capitulum diversity shows trait- and scale-specific environmental structure rather than one universal climate-associated morphology: orientation and visible colour provide the most consistent signals across analytical scales, while selected outline associations are strongest within taxa and fine involucral signals remain provisional. Sensitivity analyses grade the robustness of these spatial patterns but do not by themselves erase the global estimand. The study maps phenotypic pattern and environment–trait structure; it does not establish phenotypic plasticity, adaptation or causal mechanisms, and independent detector and trait validation remain necessary.
