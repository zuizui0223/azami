# Title and abstract

## Preferred title

**Global citizen-science images reveal continuous within-taxon variation and environmental structure in thistle capitulum traits**

## Abstract

### Aim

To test whether public photographs can recover repeated continuous capitulum phenotypes, partition variation within and among thistle taxa, and identify trait–environment associations across biological scales.

### Location

Global.

### Time period

Observations dated 1985–2026; climate normals for 1981–2010.

### Major taxa studied

Source-assigned taxa of *Cirsium* and allied Cardueae.

### Methods

An object detector localized capitula; deterministic functions measured nine continuous orientation, visible-colour and outline endpoints. A balanced atlas comprised 6,626 heads from 3,725 observations and 216 taxa; a spatially thinned cohort comprised 46,276 observations from 259 taxa. We partitioned visible variance; fitted within-taxon climate and spatial models; tested cyclic collection timing, dominant-taxon and native-range sensitivities; and compared among-taxon environmental separation with 10,000 trait-label permutations. Phylogenetic analyses were retained as Supporting Information sensitivity only.

### Results

Across endpoints, 0.589–0.931 of visible variance occurred below taxon means, and one-head-per-photograph and equal-replication sensitivities retained this pattern. All four non-circular primary rows retained sign and Benjamini–Hochberg support after both cyclic day-of-year adjustments and retained sign across predeclared dominant-taxon omissions, although magnitudes varied. Under a separately frozen native-only rule, only orientation–annual mean temperature and aspect ratio–temperature seasonality retained sign and Benjamini–Hochberg support; chroma–annual precipitation and aspect ratio–annual precipitation did not. Effects remained small. Image-resolution and sharpness adjustment removed Benjamini–Hochberg support from all three previously supported involucral rows. Among-taxon environmental separation was strongest for orientation and visible colour, but direct-backbone support narrowed.

### Main conclusions

Public photographs can preserve repeated numerical phenotypes that species means conceal, but the present variance components also contain uncontrolled imaging variation. Two small primary climate rows survived collection-timing, dominant-taxon and native-range controls, but developmental stage and repeat-photograph variance remain unresolved; colour-specific inference additionally requires a paired image-region control. The associations do not establish plasticity, adaptation or causal mechanisms, and biological accuracy requires independent detector and measurement validation.
