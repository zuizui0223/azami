# Title and abstract

## Preferred title

**Global citizen-science images reveal continuous within-taxon variation and environmental structure in thistle capitulum traits**

## Abstract

### Aim

Broad-scale trait ecology commonly reduces species to mean values or discrete trait states, thereby discarding much of the variation among repeated observations within species. Public biodiversity photographs could overcome this limitation if heterogeneous images can be converted into reproducible continuous phenotypic measurements rather than categorical labels. We asked whether global citizen-science images can recover repeated continuous thistle capitulum traits, how visible variation is distributed within and among taxa, and whether trait–environment associations occur within taxa and persist in among-taxon historical sensitivity analyses.

### Location

Global.

### Time period

Public observations dated 1985–2026; climatic predictors were 1981–2010 normals.

### Major taxa studied

Source-assigned taxa in *Cirsium* and allied Cardueae thistles.

### Methods

A frozen YOLO11n detector localized visible capitula in public photographs. YOLO was used only to define the observation region; deterministic image-processing functions then converted each assessable detected head into numerical trait measurements rather than categorical states. Nine primary continuous endpoints spanned orientation angle, visible corolla colour (CIELAB lightness, chroma and circular hue components) and two-dimensional outline geometry. A high-resolution subset additionally yielded three inferential involucre/spine-like contour proxies. The balanced image-comparison atlas contained 6,626 capitula from 3,725 observations and 216 source-assigned taxa, while the exhaustive spatially thinned primary cohort contained 46,276 observations from 259 source-assigned taxa. We retained repeated head-level values as within-taxon distributions, decomposed visible image variance among taxa, photographs and heads, fitted within-taxon climate models and grouped SPDE-INLA spatial models, tested among-taxon environmental niche contrasts against 10,000 trait-label permutations and evaluated among-taxon climate associations using Pagel-λ PGLS across alternative grafted trees. Robustness audits examined residual spatial autocorrelation, broad-region omission and simultaneous collapse of all WCVP synonym conflicts.

### Results

Across the nine primary endpoints, 0.589–0.931 of visible image variance occurred below assigned taxon means, with 0.440–0.691 among photographs within taxa and 0.143–0.379 among heads within photographs. One-head-per-photo and equal-replication sensitivities retained the same qualitative conclusion. Within taxa, orientation angle increased with annual mean temperature, corolla chroma increased with annual precipitation, outline aspect ratio increased with temperature seasonality and decreased with annual precipitation, and circular hue components varied with temperature and precipitation structure. Grouped SPDE-INLA additionally supported temperature and soil-pH associations with visible colour while providing little global BH support for gross-outline traits. In the high-resolution layer, involucre projection roughness, outward spread fraction and maximum spine-like projection all increased with temperature seasonality. Among-taxon environmental sorting exceeded permutation-null expectations mainly for orientation and visible colour. Six taxon-level climate associations were BH-supported in all 50 randomized Pagel-λ PGLS trees, but only 54 of 216 atlas taxa were direct dated-backbone tips. Residual Moran's I was near zero for every primary endpoint (-0.0096 to 0.0030; all permutation P > 0.05), broad-region omission retained taxon rankings strongly, and simultaneous collapse of eight WCVP synonym conflicts changed neither coefficient signs nor primary FDR decisions.

### Main conclusions

Citizen-science photographs can be treated not only as species records or sources of coarse trait categories, but as repeated numerical phenotype observations. Retaining continuous head-level measurements revealed substantial visible capitulum-trait variation both among and within source-assigned thistle taxa, with part of that variation structured along environmental gradients in a trait- and scale-specific manner. Orientation and visible colour showed the clearest environmental structure, whereas most gross-outline traits were weakly associated after spatial modelling; fine involucral architecture tracked temperature seasonality in the high-resolution layer. The principal methodological contribution is therefore a globally scalable route from uncontrolled public images to continuous within-taxon trait distributions. Historical interpretations remain conditional on incomplete backbone coverage, and all trait–environment relationships remain observational rather than evidence of plasticity or adaptation.

## Claim boundary

The manuscript does not claim demonstrated phenotypic plasticity, local adaptation, selection, pollinator causation, evolutionary rate, adaptive radiation, rain-protection adaptation or a resolved *Cirsium* species tree. Detector and continuous-measurement biological validity still require genuinely independent human/reference validation.
