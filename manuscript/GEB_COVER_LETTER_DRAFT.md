Dear Editors of *Global Ecology and Biogeography*,

Please consider our Research Article, **“Global citizen-science images reveal multidimensional and scale-dependent environmental organization of thistle capitula.”** Broad-scale trait syntheses commonly compress taxa to species means or coarse categories. We use globally distributed public photographs to retain repeated continuous phenotypes of one reproductive structure and ask how diversity, multivariate organization and environment alignment change from within-taxon variation to among-taxon geography. Across 46,276 observations and 259 source-assigned taxa, substantial visible variation occurs below taxon means, an expanded 18-endpoint morphospace remains strongly multidimensional, and whole-capitulum association geometry is only partly shared across biological scales. A predeclared nested comparison further shows that four conventional CHELSA variables are adequate for the current within-taxon multivariate estimand but do not exhaust among-taxon structure, for which growing-season precipitation is the most stable added representation. The paper's general contribution is a reproducible route from heterogeneous public imagery to repeated phenotype distributions and an explicit demonstration that environmental organization cannot be inferred by treating species means and within-taxon variation as interchangeable. We retain a strict non-causal boundary and evidence hierarchy for image-derived traits.
