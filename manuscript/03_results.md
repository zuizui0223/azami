# Results

## Global image phenomics recovered repeated continuous capitulum phenotypes

The balanced atlas contained 6,626 heads from 3,725 photographs and 216 taxa. The exhaustive stream contained 406,582 detector-positive observations from 286 taxa, of which 46,276 observations from 259 taxa formed the primary spatially thinned cohort. Trait-specific quality control retained 87.2% of atlas heads for colour, 80.4% for outline and 69.2% for orientation; the original high-resolution layer retained 1,443 heads from 1,292 observations and 210 taxa (Tables 1–2; Table S1.1). The independent detector-audit packet contains 1,000 source images, but human adjudication remains incomplete; detector precision and recall are therefore not reported.

The GEB v2 contract defined 27 continuous endpoints across eight measurement modules. The final artifact-backed run measured 22 endpoints. Eighteen of 19 inferential endpoints had observation-level measurements: all nine established primary endpoints and nine expanded candidate endpoints. The only unexecuted inferential endpoint was `visible_floret_fraction`; four descriptive colour-composition pixel fractions were also unexecuted. The continuous-trait universe retained 46,276 observations from 259 taxa and 374,255 analysis-eligible observation × endpoint measurements. Missing or unexecuted values remained missing rather than being converted to categorical absence or biological zero.

## Most visible variation occurred below taxon means

Starting from all 27 GEB v2 endpoints, 22 had finite measurements and five remained explicit unexecuted rows. For every measured endpoint, most raw observation-level sum of squares lay below source-assigned taxon means: the fraction ranged from 0.8137 for Lab lightness to 0.9863 for outline aspect ratio. Because the public-image cohort was strongly unbalanced among taxa, the equal-replication sensitivity sampled two observations per eligible taxon. Its endpoint-specific median below-taxon fractions ranged from 0.2869 for hue sine to 0.5846 for basal taper ratio. Thus, repeated photographs retained substantial within-taxon visible information even after equalizing taxon contributions; a one-row-per-taxon database would compress that information.

Across the nine established primary endpoints, the combined fraction of visible image sums of squares below taxon means ranged from 0.589 for hue sine to 0.931 for width-profile variation (Figure 3). Variation among photographs within taxa contributed 0.440–0.691 and variation among heads within photographs contributed 0.143–0.379. One-head-per-photograph sensitivities retained below-taxon fractions of 0.582–0.899, and medians across equal-replication sensitivities were 0.528–0.879 (Table S1.4; Figure S1.1). Thus, the below-taxon pattern was not explained solely by multiple heads in one photograph or unequal replication among taxa.

Taxon medians nevertheless retained multivariate structure. In the frozen nine-primary-endpoint PCA, PC1 explained 32.9%, PCs 1–2 explained 56.1% and PCs 1–3 explained 69.3% of variance. Visible phenotype diversity was therefore substantial at both below-taxon and among-taxon scales.

## Within-taxon environmental associations were trait specific

Eight of 36 primary endpoint-component models passed BH correction (Table S1.3; Figures 5 and S1.2). Among non-circular endpoints, orientation angle increased with annual mean temperature (standardized β = 0.0171, 95% CI 0.0046–0.0295, q = 0.0363), corolla chroma increased with annual precipitation (β = 0.0393, CI 0.0102–0.0684, q = 0.0363), outline aspect ratio increased with temperature seasonality (β = 0.0137, CI 0.0048–0.0226, q = 0.0207) and decreased with annual precipitation (β = −0.0108, CI −0.0187 to −0.0028, q = 0.0363). Four additional supported component rows involved hue sine or cosine, but individual circular components do not define named biological colours.

All four non-circular rows retained sign and BH support after predeclared taxon-specific cyclic day-of-year adjustment. The adjusted standardized coefficients were 0.0158 for orientation–BIO1 (q = 0.000107), 0.0475 for chroma–BIO12 (q = 0.0202), 0.0206 for aspect ratio–BIO4 (q = 1.54 × 10⁻⁸) and −0.0133 for aspect ratio–BIO12 (q = 0.000814). Coefficient signs also persisted after each of the ten most represented taxa was omitted and after *Cirsium vulgare* and *C. arvense*, together representing 54.0% of observations, were omitted jointly. Magnitudes varied most for chroma, whose omission estimates ranged from 0.0159 to 0.0534.

Hemisphere-aware sensitivities included 2,356 Southern Hemisphere observations. After shifting Southern Hemisphere dates by one half-cycle, β was 0.0135 for orientation–BIO1 (q = 0.00146), 0.0421 for chroma–BIO12 (q = 0.00852), 0.0178 for aspect ratio–BIO4 (q = 0.000355) and −0.0121 for aspect ratio–BIO12 (q = 0.00654). Separate taxon-specific cyclic curves by hemisphere gave the same four signs and BH decisions. These analyses address collection timing, not photographed developmental stage.

The frozen WCVP/TDWG native-range join resolved 245 of 259 source taxa. Of 46,276 observations, 27,066 were classified as native, 10,554 introduced, 5,491 unresolved at the taxon join, 2,100 outside a mapped level-3 polygon and 1,065 in a mapped unit not listed for the accepted taxon. Native-only refits retained orientation–BIO1 (β = 0.0226, q = 0.0311; 21,725 observations, 128 taxa) and aspect ratio–BIO4 (β = 0.0284, q = 1.25 × 10⁻¹⁷; 22,528 observations, 126 taxa) under the predeclared same-sign plus BH rule. Chroma–BIO12 (β = 0.0278, q = 0.0754) and aspect ratio–BIO12 (β = −0.0157, q = 0.133) retained their primary directions but failed native-only BH correction and were classified as range-sensitive.

Grouped spatial models concentrated stable support in orientation and visible colour (Table S1.5; Figure S1.3). Orientation retained a positive annual-mean-temperature association. Lightness increased with annual mean temperature and decreased with soil pH; chroma decreased with annual mean temperature and increased with annual precipitation; hue sine increased with temperature seasonality; and hue cosine decreased with annual mean temperature and increased with soil pH. No topographic effect and no spatial-model effect for aspect ratio, circularity, solidity or width-profile variation passed global BH correction. The aspect-ratio associations were therefore specific to the primary within-taxon analysis, whereas soil-pH associations emerged in the broader grouped spatial layer.

## Expanded involucral architecture separated quality-robust from image-sensitive signals

The earlier high-resolution contour screen had identified three positive BIO4 associations for projection roughness, outward spread fraction and maximum spine-like projection. After adjustment for minimum head dimension and image sharpness, none passed the predeclared confirmatory BH rule (q = 0.0696–0.0730), and roughness and maximum projection reversed sign in the 150–199-pixel stratum. These legacy contour rows remain image-quality-sensitive provenance and are not used as evidence that botanical spine length or phyllary spreading responds to temperature seasonality.

GEB v2 replaced reliance on those contour summaries with an explicitly registered expanded candidate layer. Three candidate rows passed the registry-wide climate FDR screen. Involucre length/width ratio increased with precipitation seasonality (BIO15; β = 0.054923, q = 0.000282), involucre apical taper decreased with annual precipitation (BIO12; β = −0.049721, q = 0.000821), and upper-tail bract projection (`bract_projection_p95`) decreased with precipitation seasonality (BIO15; β = −0.033673, q = 0.014377).

Endpoint-specific models adjusted for resolution and sharpness and were repeated within fixed resolution strata. The length/width–BIO15 and apical-taper–BIO12 signals retained direction after quality adjustment and in all successful strata, so both were graded `C_exploratory_quality_robust`. The `bract_projection_p95`–BIO15 association showed sign instability among resolution strata and was graded `C_exploratory_image_sensitive`. Six of 36 quality-adjusted candidate climate rows passed FDR, but only the three registry-wide candidate signals enter the evidence atlas; the adjusted count is a sensitivity result, not six headline discoveries. All candidate geometry remains image derived pending independent botanical calibration.

## Matched within- and among-taxon analyses revealed scale-specific environmental alignment

The 18 measured endpoints form 17 inferential units after circular hue is treated jointly. Across the same four frozen CHELSA predictors, the main >=5 among-taxon scope contained 68 endpoint × predictor models. Four rows passed among-taxon FDR, all in the established primary tier; no candidate endpoint passed among-taxon FDR.

When matched to the established within-taxon family, the 68 rows classified as three `both_scales`, eight `within_only`, one `among_only` and 56 `neither`. Orientation was scale specific: its established within-taxon association was with BIO1, whereas its among-taxon association was with BIO12. Joint circular hue contained the clearest partial cross-scale support. All three final candidate atlas rows—length/width–BIO15, apical taper–BIO12 and `bract_projection_p95`–BIO15—were `within_only`.

The expanded common-taxon environmental-sorting analysis retained 44 taxa at the >=5 threshold. No linear endpoint passed both FDR sorting metrics; joint circular hue was supported. At the >=2 threshold, 78 taxa were retained, orientation was the only linear endpoint passing both centroid-separation and overlap FDR, and joint hue was supported. No candidate linear endpoint passed expanded among-taxon sorting at either threshold.

## The full-27 / full-environment atlas resolved endpoint, gradient and scale

All 46,276 coordinate-bearing observations were joined to BIO1, BIO4, BIO12, BIO15, shortwave radiation, VPD, wind, growing-season precipitation and potential NPP. Eight predictors had complete coverage and wind had 99.989% coverage. The 22 measured endpoints occupied 190-431 five-degree cells, depending on measurement layer. All 1,874 observations overlapping the earlier complete-18 process artifact reproduced the nine environmental values exactly.

The within-taxon atlas contained 189 executed tests and 45 explicit unexecuted rows. Twenty-six executed rows passed one global BH family. The supported set spanned every environmental block: thermal associations included orientation-BIO1 and outline aspect-ratio-BIO4; hydric associations included apical-taper-BIO12 and involucre length/width-BIO15; radiative/atmospheric associations included chroma-VPD, projection roughness-radiation and projection-peak-density-VPD; GSP aligned with seven involucral or surface proxies; basal taper aligned with NPP; and joint hue aligned with seven gradients distributed across the six blocks. These results did not define one whole-head environmental syndrome.

The primary among-taxon min5 scope contained 180 successful tests, 45 unexecuted rows and nine no-finite-variation rows for the surface specular proxy. Ten rows passed the separate global BH family: orientation with BIO12 and GSP; chroma with radiation; projection-peak density with VPD; and joint hue with BIO1, BIO4, BIO15, radiation, wind and NPP. Seven rows repeated in the min2 scope. In the matched min5 grid, seven rows were supported at both scales, 18 within taxa only, three among taxa only, 152 at neither scale and 54 were not comparable. The among-only rows were orientation-BIO12, orientation-GSP and chroma-radiation. Most supported involucral and surface-proxy rows were within-only.

The underlying sample was not geographically or taxonomically balanced. Europe and North America contributed 50.43% and 41.83% of the 46,276 observations, respectively, and 94.91% of observations were in the Northern Hemisphere. *Cirsium vulgare* and *C. arvense* contributed 28.54% and 25.49%; the top ten taxa contributed 79.93%. The retrospective sampling-composition audit evaluated all 36 global-q-supported pairs under 674 declared perturbations, and every scenario was evaluable. All ten selected among-taxon pairs retained direction under every dominant-taxon, region-omission and native-only scenario. Sixteen of 26 within-taxon pairs retained direction under every scenario, whereas ten reversed in at least one. Reversals occurred in five equal-taxon-weight checks, three dominant-taxon omissions, three North-America omissions and two native-only checks, with some pairs failing more than one family. These are directional sensitivities, not new significance tests.

The new broad-space screen admitted only the 26 within-taxon and ten among-taxon q-supported rows. Five within-taxon pairs met all three passage conditions: orientation-BIO1 (spatial beta = 0.0265, permutation P = 0.002, residual Moran P = 0.324), chroma-VPD (-0.0421, P = 0.001, Moran P = 0.381), involucre length/width-BIO15 (0.0602, P = 0.022, Moran P = 0.411), projection roughness-radiation (-0.1392, P = 0.005, Moran P = 0.626) and surface high-frequency energy-BIO1 (0.1235, P = 0.026, Moran P = 0.288). Joint hue retained several spatial-basis effects, but every hue row retained detectable residual spatial autocorrelation and therefore failed the complete gate.

Four of those five broad-space-retained within-taxon pairs also retained direction in every sampling-composition scenario. Projection roughness-radiation did not: its negative atlas coefficient became positive both under equal total taxon weighting and when *C. vulgare* plus *C. arvense* were omitted jointly. It therefore remains a broad-space pass but is explicitly sampling-composition sensitive.

Two among-taxon rows passed the broad-space gate: chroma decreased with radiation (spatial beta = -0.7124, P = 0.001, residual Moran P = 0.217), and the signed head-axis angle relative to EXIF-image vertical increased with BIO12 (beta = 0.2861, P = 0.017, Moran P = 0.082). Both were then successful, significant and directionally stable on all 52 historical-placement trees. Chroma-radiation retained P = 0.000024 on every tree; orientation-BIO12 retained P = 0.000201-0.000231. These two rows were labelled adaptive-pattern candidates under current controls. The other rows remained supported, unsupported or unresolved present-geography results rather than being deleted.

Both candidate rows also retained direction in all 18 applicable sampling-composition scenarios. Their smallest absolute effect-magnitude ratios relative to the atlas coefficient were 0.615 for chroma-radiation and 0.781 for orientation-BIO12, occurring under broad-region omission. This adds directional sampling-composition stability but does not change the retrospective candidate label or make the sample globally representative.

## A process-based environmental extension was required among taxa but not within taxa

The complete-18 environmental cohort retained all 1,874 observations and 124 taxa. CHELSA coverage was 100% for mean shortwave radiation, mean vapour-pressure deficit, mean near-surface wind, growing-season precipitation and potential NPP. Variable identities and block definitions were frozen before process-extension outcomes were inspected.

Six predeclared environmental blocks were first tested separately against the 18D response. No block passed the six-block BH family at either biological scale or replication threshold. The main within-taxon thermal block was nominally supported (R² = 0.008510, P = 0.01510) but did not pass six-block BH correction (Q = 0.09059). Stand-alone block tests were therefore not promoted as new evidence-atlas discoveries.

The predeclared nested comparison then asked whether the five process variables added information beyond BIO1, BIO4, BIO12 and BIO15. Within taxa, the omnibus extension was unsupported at both thresholds: at >=5, delta R² = 0.013350, partial R² = 0.013535 and P = 0.24468; at >=2, delta R² = 0.016314, partial R² = 0.016512 and P = 0.13079. No within-taxon block-specific extension passed BH. The frozen four-variable core was therefore adequate for the current within-taxon whole-capitulum estimand.

Among taxa, the omnibus extension was supported at both thresholds. At >=5, core R² was 0.079680, full R² 0.277528, delta R² 0.197849, partial R² 0.214978 and P = 0.000700. At >=2, core R² was 0.069350, full R² 0.156691, delta R² 0.087340, partial R² 0.093849 and P = 0.03020. Growing-season precipitation was the only block-specific increment supported at both thresholds: partial R² = 0.078652 and Q = 0.003200 at >=5, and partial R² = 0.031017 and Q = 0.031997 at >=2. Radiation plus VPD and potential NPP were supported only in the main >=5 scope; wind was unsupported at both thresholds.

Thus, BIO1/BIO4/BIO12/BIO15 were an adequate low-dimensional representation for the current within-taxon 18D analysis but did not exhaust among-taxon environmental structure. Growing-season precipitation was the most replication-stable added environmental representation. This nested result diagnoses observational redundancy and does not establish a direct causal precipitation process.

## Whole-capitulum structure provided a secondary synthesis

Expanding the trait universe changed its dimensionality rather than revealing one finer master syndrome. Among 127 taxa complete for all 18 measured inferential endpoints, PC1 explained 18.49%, PC2 12.01% and PC3 11.78%, for 42.28% cumulative variance (Figure 4). Within the seven-endpoint involucre-architecture module, PC1, PC2 and PC3 explained 37.13%, 15.59% and 14.24%, respectively. The expanded trait universe therefore resolved several partly independent dimensions of capitulum phenotype.

The same 18 endpoints were complete for 1,874 observations from 124 taxa. The main whole-capitulum scope retained 1,734 observations from 42 taxa with at least five complete observations per taxon; the >=2 sensitivity retained 1,825 observations from 75 taxa. Treating circular hue jointly gave 17 inferential units.

Within registered measurement modules, association strength exceeded between-module association strength at both biological scales. In the main scope, the within-taxon module contrast was 0.164502 (taxon-bootstrap 95% CI 0.130693–0.179475), and the among-taxon contrast was 0.088475 (CI 0.024942–0.126171). The within- and among-taxon 17-unit association matrices were positively but incompletely related (Spearman 0.366299, CI 0.118457–0.399817). The >=2 sensitivity gave the same qualitative result: within contrast 0.157688, among contrast 0.083662 and cross-scale Spearman 0.377272. These results reject one universal syndrome and support partial phenotypic / measurement-module organization, but they do not replace the endpoint-level geography or establish functional or genetic modularity.

## Robustness analyses graded rather than erased the observed pattern

Residual Moran's I ranged from −0.0096 to 0.0030, with no permutation P < 0.05 (Table S1.9; Figure S1.4). Omitting one broad geographic region at a time retained taxon rankings strongly, with minimum endpoint-specific Spearman ρ of 0.856–0.972 (Figure S1.5). Simultaneously collapsing eight WCVP synonym conflicts reduced the atlas from 216 to 211 operational units and the primary cohort from 259 to 251, but all eight primary BH-supported component rows remained supported, no coefficient changed sign and the largest absolute β change was 0.000385 (Table S1.10).

The source metadata also yielded a frozen repeat-photo cohort of 20,073 observations from 236 taxa, comprising 58,748 public photographs and 38,675 photographs beyond the first. Trait remeasurement is pending, so no repeat-photo variance estimate is reported.

Six taxon-level climate associations were retained across all 50 randomized-tree PGLS fits in the historical-placement sensitivity: orientation–annual precipitation, hue sine–annual mean temperature, hue sine–temperature seasonality, hue sine–annual precipitation, solidity–temperature seasonality and width-profile variation–annual mean temperature. However, only 54 of 216 atlas taxa were direct dated-backbone tips, most taxa required within-genus placement and Pagel λ was estimated as zero. These coefficients remain Supporting Information sensitivities to tested placements rather than definitive phylogenetically independent effects.

## The evidence atlas retained distinct claim levels

The final evidence atlas assigned orientation–BIO1 and aspect ratio–BIO4 to `A_robust`; retained range-sensitive and colour-calibration-dependent primary patterns as B-grade evidence; and separated the expanded candidate layer into two quality-robust C-grade rows and one image-sensitive C-grade row. The new whole-capitulum and process-environment analyses form a separate multivariate pattern layer and do not promote or demote the endpoint-level A/B/C grades.

Collectively, the results show extensive visible diversity below taxon means, trait-specific environmental associations, different within/among scale classes and a richer environmental representation required among taxa but not within taxa. The high-dimensional morphospace and partial whole-capitulum organization synthesize those decomposed results without replacing them.
