# Discussion

## Species means are incomplete descriptions of visible capitulum phenotype

Across the nine primary endpoints, 59–93% of total visible image sums of squares occurred below assigned taxon means, and this conclusion survived both one-head-per-photo and equal-replication sensitivities. Most of the below-taxon component occurred among photographs rather than among multiple heads within one photograph. Public photographs inevitably combine biological heterogeneity with viewpoint, illumination, developmental stage and measurement error, so these fractions are not genetic variance estimates. Their importance is distributional: a single taxon mean discards a large and structured portion of the observed phenotype.

Substantial among-taxon structure remained at the same time. The taxon-level PCA required several components to describe orientation, colour and outline, showing that thistle capitulum phenotype is multidimensional rather than one-dimensional. The combined result is therefore not that taxon means are uninformative, but that trait architecture exists simultaneously at several nested scales.

## Public photographs can preserve continuous within-taxon phenotype rather than only categories

The methodological advance is not simply the number of photographs processed. Object detection supplied a reproducible observation unit, after which each assessable capitulum was converted into continuous numerical phenotype—angles, colour coordinates and contour statistics—rather than a small set of predefined states. Retaining these repeated values made the within-taxon distribution itself an analysable object.

This matters because broad comparative datasets commonly compress taxa into one or a few characteristic values, while image-classification approaches can likewise discard intermediate states by forcing morphology into bins. Here, the same public-image resource could instead be used to quantify how phenotype is distributed within taxa, how much information is lost by the taxon mean and whether continuous within-taxon deviations covary with environment at global extent.

The framework is intentionally modular rather than morphologically exhaustive. Conclusions apply to the measured visible capitulum phenotype, and additional characters should enter the framework only when image assessability and independent validation are sufficient.

## Environmental associations were trait specific

Orientation showed the clearest temperature signal within taxa: warmer locations were associated with larger image-referenced head angles, and this direction was retained across all four SPDE predictor groups. Visible colour was associated with several environmental dimensions. Lightness increased with annual mean temperature and decreased with soil pH; chroma decreased with temperature in the SPDE analysis but increased with annual precipitation in both the exhaustive and spatial models; and hue components varied with temperature structure, precipitation and soil pH. Because hue is circular and camera-recorded, these coefficients describe movement in circular colour coordinates rather than named pollinator-perceived colours.

Gross outline showed a different pattern. Aspect ratio increased with temperature seasonality and decreased with annual precipitation in the exhaustive within-taxon analysis, but these effects did not pass global BH screening in the SPDE models. Circularity, solidity and width-profile CV showed no multiplicity-supported within-taxon environmental relationship. Thus, visible shape variation was large, yet much of it was not organized along the environmental gradients that produced the clearest orientation and colour signals.

The high-resolution involucral layer extended the same framework to finer bract architecture. Roughness, outward spread and maximum spine-like projection all increased with temperature seasonality in the ≤10 km cohort. Their coherence is notable because three related contour summaries point in the same direction, but the standardized effects were modest and explained only about 0.8–1.4% of variation. The algorithm also cannot separate a long spine from a spreading or recurved bract. The supported interpretation is therefore a small association between temperature seasonality and outward involucral contour architecture, not a demonstrated defensive adaptation.

## Among-taxon sorting and historical sensitivity answer a different question

The environmental-niche permutation test showed that the strongest separation of low- and high-trait taxa was concentrated in orientation and visible colour rather than being a generic consequence of quartile splitting; most gross-outline traits showed little support. Six climate associations were also FDR-supported across all 50 randomized Pagel-λ PGLS trees.

These trees are historical sensitivity devices rather than a resolved species phylogeny. Three quarters of the atlas taxa are grafted, and *Cirsium* is affected by hybridization, incomplete lineage sorting and polyploidy. The appropriate conclusion is therefore that some among-taxon environmental structure is stronger than permutation expectation and several coefficients are insensitive to the tested grafting placements, not that shared ancestry has been definitively removed.

The contrast between scales is biologically informative. Within-taxon associations emphasize annual mean temperature for orientation, precipitation for chroma and aspect ratio, and temperature seasonality for involucral architecture. The among-taxon PGLS screen emphasizes a partly different set, including annual precipitation for orientation and temperature seasonality for solidity. This mismatch is expected if within-taxon spatial variation, taxon turnover and historical differentiation represent different processes. It also argues against treating one taxon-level environmental relationship as a substitute for population-level trait variation.

## Why *Cirsium* is a stringent system for this framework

The same capitulum expresses conspicuous diversity in orientation, visible colour, gross outline and involucral architecture, allowing several phenotype modules to be measured on one reproductive unit. The lineage also includes recent regional radiations and a complex evolutionary history involving hybridization, incomplete lineage sorting, polyploidy and phenotypic convergence. This combination makes *Cirsium* especially informative for asking whether trait diversity is structured at the level of repeated observations, among taxa or both.

The present analysis does not demonstrate that *Cirsium* is an adaptive radiation. Rapid regional diversification is evolutionary context, not a result inferred from the image data. Resolved transition histories, reticulation and causal mechanisms belong to the downstream EAzami and focal empirical programs.

## Completed robustness audits narrow the main computational concerns

Residual spatial autocorrelation was negligible after the accepted SPDE specifications, broad-region omission retained taxon rankings strongly and simultaneous WCVP synonym collapse left the primary FDR pattern and nested-variance conclusion unchanged. We therefore retain source-platform assignments as transparent operational taxonomic units and report checklist lumping as sensitivity rather than silently rewriting observation identities.

The environmental-niche permutation analysis also showed that the strongest among-taxon sorting was not a generic artifact of quartile splitting. Together, these audits close the major repository-computable spatial, niche-null and taxonomic robustness gates for the frozen analysis.

## The remaining weakness is independent measurement validity, not another statistical model

Two validation issues remain genuinely external to the repository. First, the production detector was developed from pseudo-labels and still requires adjudicated independent human boxes. Second, continuous orientation, colour and outline require independent reference measurements. Until those audits are complete, internal detector metrics, QC retention and horizontal-mirror repeatability must not be described as biological accuracy.

This limitation matters particularly because the study's novelty depends on treating public photographs as repeated numerical phenotype observations. If orientation angles, colour coordinates or outline statistics are systematically biased by image conditions, the inferred within-taxon distributions could be distorted. The correct final step is therefore independent measurement validation and, if needed, propagation of observed error into the headline sensitivities—not further expansion of the statistical model set.

## Overall implication

The results support a multiscale and distributional view of floral phenotype. Taxon means capture real among-taxon differences, but they coexist with substantial variation among repeated numerical observations within taxa. Environmental associations are detectable at both scales yet differ among orientation, colour, gross outline and involucral architecture, and their strength depends on spatial and historical treatment.

The central implication is broader than a set of *Cirsium* climate correlations: globally distributed public photographs can preserve continuous within-taxon phenotype that would otherwise be discarded by species averaging or coarse categorization. Comparative trait ecology can benefit from retaining these distributions, observation hierarchy and analysis scale rather than reducing repeated phenotypes to one value or one state per taxon.

## Legacy lability result

The former negative relation between visible variation and raw absolute taxon-specific environmental slopes was a precision artefact. After slope uncertainty was modelled, no common cross-module coupling remained. That correction is retained as statistical provenance and a methodological warning, not as the current biological story.
