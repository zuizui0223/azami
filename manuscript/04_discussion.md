# Discussion

## Species means are incomplete descriptions of visible capitulum phenotype

Across the nine primary endpoints, 59–93% of total visible image sums of squares occurred below assigned taxon means, and this conclusion survived both one-head-per-photo and equal-replication sensitivities. Most of the below-taxon component occurred among photographs rather than among multiple heads within one photograph. Public photographs inevitably combine biological heterogeneity with viewpoint, illumination, developmental stage and measurement error, so these fractions are not genetic variance estimates. Their importance is distributional: a single taxon mean discards a large and structured portion of the observed phenotype. This directly extends the argument from trait-based ecology that intraspecific variation can occupy a substantial share of ecological trait space and that its importance depends on trait and scale (Messier et al. 2010; Albert et al. 2011; Bolnick et al. 2011; Violle et al. 2012; Siefert et al. 2015).

Substantial among-taxon structure remained at the same time. The taxon-level PCA required several components to describe orientation, colour and outline, showing that thistle capitulum phenotype is multidimensional rather than one-dimensional. The combined result is therefore not that taxon means are uninformative, but that thistle reproductive phenotype is structured simultaneously at several nested scales. For *Cirsium*, the ecological unit represented by one species mean can consequently miss geographically repeated phenotype structure that is visible within the same source-assigned taxon.

## Public photographs can preserve continuous within-taxon phenotype rather than only categories

The methodological advance is not simply the number of photographs processed. Object detection supplied a reproducible observation unit, after which each assessable capitulum was converted into continuous numerical phenotype—angles, colour coordinates and contour statistics—rather than a small set of predefined states. Retaining these repeated values made the within-taxon distribution itself an analysable object. This complements global trait databases, which are indispensable for cross-species synthesis but often require aggregation of unevenly replicated measurements, and recent image-based trait inference, which demonstrates that citizen-science imagery can support continuous trait estimation at large scales (Kattge et al. 2011, 2020; Maitner et al. 2018; Sharma et al. 2026).

The framework is intentionally modular rather than morphologically exhaustive. Conclusions apply to measured visible capitulum phenotype, and additional characters should enter only when image assessability and independent validation are sufficient. That boundary is especially important here because the ecological interpretation becomes most interesting for exactly those traits—orientation, colour and involucral architecture—for which camera geometry, illumination or two-dimensional projection could otherwise be mistaken for direct biological measurements.

## Capitulum orientation links the strongest within-taxon climate signal to reproductive microclimate and exposure

Orientation showed the clearest temperature signal within taxa: warmer locations were associated with larger image-referenced head angles, and the positive BIO1 direction was retained across all four grouped SPDE predictor sets. This result is ecological pattern rather than mechanism, but it identifies capitulum orientation as a plausible interface between *Cirsium* reproduction and the physical environment.

Flowers can differ substantially from ambient air in temperature, and floral thermal balance depends on radiation, convection, morphology, colour and orientation; these temperature differences can affect pollen and ovule function, floral development and pollinator behaviour (van der Kooi et al. 2019). A direct orientation manipulation in sunflower, another Asteraceae, altered floral temperature, the timing of pollen presentation and stigma receptivity, pollinator visitation and components of reproductive performance (Creux et al. 2021). Particularly relevant to the present system, Herrera (2025) showed that summer-blooming Cardueae—including *Cirsium* species—often maintain capitula cooler than surrounding air under hot Mediterranean conditions, with cooling increasing at high ambient temperature. Thistle capitula therefore possess a demonstrable thermal ecology, making the global BIO1–orientation association biologically meaningful even though the present images do not measure floral temperature.

Orientation can also regulate exposure to precipitation and ultraviolet radiation rather than temperature alone. In the alpine Asteraceae *Cremanthodium campanulatum*, natural nodding did not principally operate through warming; instead, nodding and involucral shielding reduced exposure of reproductive tissues to rain and UV-B, and experimentally erect heads had substantially lower achene set without evidence that pollinators preferred the natural orientation (Chen et al. 2013). These studies imply several non-exclusive mechanisms—thermal regulation, rain/UV protection and floral presentation—that could generate environment-dependent orientation. Our data cannot distinguish among them, and image vertical is not a measurement of gravitational inclination or solar azimuth. The supported new ecological inference is narrower: **capitulum orientation in thistles is not merely a taxonomic shape descriptor; within taxa it is globally structured along temperature gradients in a direction robust to spatial modelling, making reproductive microclimate and exposure explicit mechanistic targets for field validation.**

## Visible colour integrates abiotic and biotic axes rather than one pigment-stress gradient

Visible colour showed the broadest set of environmentally structured responses after spatial modelling. Lightness increased with annual mean temperature and decreased with soil pH; chroma decreased with temperature in the grouped SPDE models but increased with annual precipitation in both the exhaustive and spatial analyses; and circular hue components varied with temperature seasonality, precipitation structure and soil pH. These results should not be compressed into a single “darker versus paler” axis because hue is circular and camera-recorded CIELAB values are neither direct pigment concentrations nor pollinator visual coordinates.

The literature nevertheless provides a strong ecological reason to expect floral colour to integrate multiple environmental processes. Climate can alter floral metabolism and reproductive signalling broadly (Borghi et al. 2019), and across floras flower-colour distributions are shaped by both biotic and abiotic correlates rather than pollinators alone (Dalrymple et al. 2020). Reviews of flower-colour polymorphism identify pollinator-mediated selection, non-pollinator agents, gene flow and drift as interacting mechanisms (Narbona et al. 2018). Experimental work in *Boechera stricta* shows that drought, soil conditions and herbivory can covary with or causally influence floral pigmentation even where pollinator selection is weak (Vaidya et al. 2018). Long-term analyses of anthocyanin polymorphisms likewise show that temperature and aridity can produce different trajectories in floral pigmentation (Sullivan & Koski 2021). Especially close to the present observational design, Grossenbacher et al. (2026) combined field sampling with iNaturalist records to show that soil substrate, climatic water deficit and UV environment contribute to geographic flower-colour polymorphism in *Leptosiphon parviflorus*. Together these studies make clear that “environmental stress” is not a single colour-selection axis and that soil and climate deserve explicit consideration alongside pollinators.

*Cirsium* itself provides unusually relevant precedents. In *C. palustre*, purple, white and intermediate flower-colour morphs vary within species; classic field analyses related morph frequencies to exposed environments and found preferential pollination of the uncommon white morph under some local conditions (Mogford 1974a,b). More recently, Chang et al. (2026) linked flower-colour polymorphism in the Taiwanese *C. japonicum* complex to anthocyanin expression and pollinator preference while also showing that colour alone does not map cleanly onto evolutionary lineage boundaries. The present global analysis moves beyond discrete morphs by retaining continuous visible colour coordinates across many thistle taxa and populations. The fact that temperature, precipitation and soil pH associate with different colour components suggests that *Cirsium* flower colour is unlikely to be explained by one universal anthocyanin-stress gradient. Instead, abiotic optical/pigment responses and biotic signalling are plausible overlapping axes whose relative importance may vary among populations and regions.

This interpretation also makes the annual-precipitation–chroma association informative rather than awkward. A simple expectation that harsher or drier sites should always carry more strongly pigmented flowers would not predict all of the observed directions. The mismatch argues for targeted measurements—spectral reflectance, pigment chemistry, floral temperature and local pollinator assemblages—rather than post hoc assignment of one adaptive colour mechanism. Thus, the ecological contribution is not that a particular named colour is favoured by a particular climate, but that **continuous within-taxon visible colour in thistles is structured by several environmental dimensions that map onto distinct candidate functions.**

## Involucral architecture raises an antagonist and protection hypothesis but does not establish defence

The high-resolution route revealed a second type of environmental structure. In the ≤10 km cohort, projection roughness, outward spread fraction and maximum spine-like projection all increased with temperature seasonality. Three related but non-identical contour summaries therefore changed coherently along BIO4, while gross capitulum-outline traits generally lost global support in the spatial models. This points to environmental structure in fine outward involucral architecture rather than a generic change in whole-head shape.

A defence hypothesis is biologically plausible because structural traits such as spinescence can reduce herbivore access across plants (Hanley et al. 2007), and *Cirsium* capitula can experience substantial predispersal seed predation. In *C. canescens*, native insect seed predators remove a meaningful fraction of developing seeds and can have demographic and evolutionary consequences (Lamp & McCarty 1982; Rose et al. 2005). In the threatened *C. pitcheri*, infested capitula produced about 60% fewer mature seeds than uninfested capitula (Gijsman et al. 2020). Mutualist and antagonist effects can also act on the same reproductive display: flower production in *C. purpuratum* alters pollinator visitation and predispersal seed predation, and bumblebee probing does not scale linearly with display size (Ohashi & Yahara 1998, 2000, 2002), while *C. arvense* floral scent attracts both pollinators and floral herbivores and its emission pattern has been interpreted in the context of that conflict (Theis 2006; Theis et al. 2007).

But direct *Cirsium* evidence also warns against assigning a defensive function simply because a structure appears protective. Sticky inflorescence exudates in *C. discolor* were explicitly tested as a possible seed-predator defence, yet neutralizing the stickiness did not support either a direct defence or a predator-attraction mechanism (Thomas 2003). Our image-derived “spine-like” variables are even further removed from function: they cannot distinguish longer bract tips from greater spreading or recurvature, and they do not measure stiffness, damage or herbivore exclusion. The BIO4 association therefore supports a **seasonality-associated outward-involucre architecture** pattern, not a defence adaptation. It motivates competing field hypotheses—antagonist exclusion, rain/UV shielding and reproductive microclimate regulation—that can be separated experimentally.

## Environmental structure is modular across capitulum traits rather than one thistle syndrome

Taken together, the environmental results do not describe a single “warm-climate” or “dry-climate” thistle morphology. Orientation retained a robust temperature association; visible colour was structured by temperature, precipitation and soil pH; most gross-outline endpoints showed little global BH support after spatial control; and fine involucral architecture tracked temperature seasonality in the high-resolution subset. The same capitulum therefore contains phenotype modules with markedly different environmental signatures.

That modularity is an ecological result in its own right. A conspicuous taxonomic difference need not be the trait most tightly associated with environmental variation, and large visible variance need not imply a strong climate coefficient. Conversely, a finer structural component can retain a coherent environmental relationship even when whole-head outline does not. This pattern argues against treating capitulum architecture as one integrated adaptive syndrome and instead suggests that orientation, visible colour, gross outline and involucral projections can respond semi-independently to different ecological pressures.

The genus provides evolutionary context for that interpretation. New World *Cirsium* combines high ecological diversity with low rDNA divergence (Kelch & Baldwin 2003), and North American analyses identify precipitation and semiaridity as important correlates of a rapid continent-wide radiation (Siniscalchi et al. 2023). The Carduus–Cirsium complex also shows difficult generic delimitation and repeated phenotypic convergence (Ackerfield et al. 2020), while hybrid/polyploid origins occur within the group (Bureš et al. 2024). Recent genus-wide phylogenomics identifies major dispersal events and rapid regional radiations, including in Japan and North America, alongside ecological specialization (Moreyra et al. 2025), and East Asian phylotranscriptomics demonstrates lineage complexity associated with island fragmentation and genome-size change (Chang et al. 2026). Our data do not test whether modular capitulum traits caused diversification. They instead supply a new empirical observation that can be taken into those evolutionary analyses: **global thistle capitulum diversity is environmentally structured in a trait-specific, modular way rather than as one common morphological axis.**

## Within-taxon and among-taxon patterns describe different ecological processes

The environmental-niche permutation test showed that among-taxon separation was concentrated in orientation and visible colour rather than being a generic consequence of quartile splitting; most gross-outline traits showed little support. Six climate associations were also FDR-supported across all 50 randomized Pagel-λ PGLS trees. However, the predictor–trait combinations at the among-taxon scale did not simply repeat the within-taxon results. Within taxa, annual mean temperature was most consistent for orientation, precipitation for chroma, and temperature seasonality for fine involucral architecture; the among-taxon PGLS screen instead included annual precipitation for orientation and temperature seasonality for solidity.

This mismatch is expected if repeated spatial variation within taxa, turnover among taxa and historical differentiation represent different ecological processes. It cautions against substituting a species-level climate relationship for a population-level one, a distinction central to trait-based ecology (Messier et al. 2010; Albert et al. 2011; Violle et al. 2012). The randomized trees are also historical sensitivity devices rather than a resolved species phylogeny: only 54 of 216 atlas taxa are direct dated-backbone tips. The appropriate conclusion is therefore that some among-taxon environmental structure exceeds permutation expectation and several coefficients are insensitive to the tested grafting placements, not that shared ancestry has been definitively removed.

## Completed robustness audits narrow the main computational concerns

Residual spatial autocorrelation was negligible after the accepted SPDE specifications, broad-region omission retained taxon rankings strongly and simultaneous WCVP synonym collapse left the primary FDR pattern and nested-variance conclusion unchanged. We therefore retain source-platform assignments as transparent operational taxonomic units and report checklist lumping as sensitivity rather than silently rewriting observation identities.

The environmental-niche permutation analysis also showed that the strongest among-taxon sorting was not a generic artifact of quartile splitting. Together, these audits close the major repository-computable spatial, niche-null and taxonomic robustness gates for the frozen analysis. They do not, however, convert cross-sectional associations into causal ecological effects.

## The remaining weakness is independent measurement validity, not another statistical model

Two validation issues remain genuinely external to the repository. First, the production detector was developed from pseudo-labels and still requires adjudicated independent human boxes. Second, continuous orientation, colour and outline require independent reference measurements. Until those audits are complete, internal detector metrics, QC retention and horizontal-mirror repeatability must not be described as biological accuracy.

This limitation matters particularly because the study's novelty depends on treating public photographs as repeated numerical phenotype observations. If orientation angles, colour coordinates or outline statistics are systematically biased by image conditions, the inferred within-taxon distributions could be distorted. The correct final step is therefore independent measurement validation and, if needed, propagation of observed error into the headline sensitivities—not further expansion of the statistical model set.

## Overall implication

The results support a multiscale and modular view of thistle reproductive phenotype. Taxon means capture real among-taxon differences, but they coexist with substantial variation among repeated numerical observations within taxa. More importantly, the environmental structure of that variation differs among orientation, visible colour, gross outline and involucral architecture. Existing ecological studies show that these modules plausibly interface with floral microclimate, rain and UV exposure, pollinator attraction and antagonist costs, but no single mechanism explains the complete pattern.

The central biological implication is therefore more specific than “*Cirsium* traits correlate with climate”: **thistle capitula behave as composite reproductive phenotypes whose component traits are environmentally structured at different scales and along different environmental axes.** The methodological implication follows from that biological result. Globally distributed public photographs can preserve continuous within-taxon phenotype that would otherwise be discarded by species averaging or coarse categorization, allowing comparative trait ecology to ask not only how taxa differ, but which components of a reproductive structure vary within taxa and where those components align with environment.

## Legacy lability result

The former negative relation between visible variation and raw absolute taxon-specific environmental slopes was a precision artefact. After slope uncertainty was modelled, no common cross-module coupling remained. That correction is retained as statistical provenance and a methodological warning, not as the current biological story.
