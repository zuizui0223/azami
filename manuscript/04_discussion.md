# Discussion

## From trait categories to quantitative phenotypic geography

The main contribution of this study is a change in the unit of broad-scale trait description. Rather than representing each thistle taxon by one categorical state or one mean value, public photographs were converted into repeated continuous measurements of visible capitulum phenotype. Across every established primary endpoint, at least 58.9% of visible image variance occurred below taxon means, and this conclusion persisted when one head was retained per photograph and replication was balanced among taxa. Taxon medians nevertheless retained several multivariate axes. Species-level summaries therefore remain informative, but they are incomplete descriptions of the phenotype visible across geography.

The GEB v2 expansion makes that point more stringent. The frozen contract defines 27 continuous endpoints across eight phenotype modules, of which 18 inferential endpoints were measured in the completed run. Among the 127 taxa complete across all 18 measured inferential endpoints, the first three principal components explained only 42.3% of variance. Adding finer involucral and armature geometry therefore did not reveal one hidden master axis. Instead, it resolved a more explicitly multidimensional capitulum morphospace.

This result extends the argument that within-species variation can be a substantial component of ecological trait variation (Messier et al. 2010; Albert et al. 2011; Bolnick et al. 2011; Violle et al. 2012; Siefert et al. 2015) from conventional trait measurements to repeated numerical measurements extracted from global public imagery. The important point is not that public photographs provide error-free biological measurements. Rather, once assessability and measurement scope are explicit, they can reveal a continuous phenotypic landscape that coarse states and taxon means would otherwise compress away.

The below-taxon component combines biological heterogeneity with uncontrolled image conditions. It should not be interpreted as heritability, plasticity or genetic variance. One-head-per-photograph analysis removes head multiplicity but does not separate camera, illumination, viewpoint or developmental-stage effects. Metadata show that 20,073 primary-cohort observations have at least two public photographs, providing a viable repeat-photo cohort for quantifying part of that imaging contribution. Thus, the present variance decomposition establishes repeated visible phenotypic diversity and identifies how much information is lost by taxon averaging; it does not yet partition biological from photographic variance completely.

## Environmental structure is trait- and scale-specific

The environmental results describe pattern rather than mechanism. In the frozen global within-taxon analysis, significant structure involved orientation, visible colour and outline aspect ratio. Grouped spatial models concentrated support in orientation and visible colour, whereas most gross-outline endpoints showed little global BH support after spatial modelling. Among taxa, permutation-supported environmental separation was again concentrated mainly in orientation and visible colour. The expanded candidate layer added finer involucral geometry associated with precipitation amount or seasonality, but those associations occupy a lower evidence tier because botanical calibration is incomplete. The same reproductive structure therefore does not map onto environment as one integrated warm-, wet- or dry-climate morphology.

This heterogeneity is itself an ecological result. Different capitulum components occupy different parts of phenotype–environment space, and their apparent importance depends on the biological scale of comparison. A trait can show a within-taxon spatial gradient without strong among-taxon sorting, or vice versa. Consequently, the appropriate synthesis is not a list of isolated significant coefficients, but a quantitative map of which phenotype modules are environmentally structured, at which scales and with what degree of robustness.

## Orientation shows the clearest cross-analysis environmental signal

Orientation showed the clearest agreement across within-taxon approaches: image-referenced head angle increased with annual mean temperature in both the primary and grouped spatial analyses. The direction persisted after raw-calendar and hemisphere-aware cyclic day-of-year adjustment, dominant-taxon omission and native-range restriction. These sensitivities make orientation–temperature one of the strongest concrete environmental patterns in the study.

Floral orientation can alter absorbed radiation, flower temperature, rain exposure, pollen and stigma timing, pollinator presentation and reproductive performance (Chen et al. 2013; van der Kooi et al. 2019; Creux et al. 2021). Cardueae capitula can also remain cooler than air under hot summer conditions (Herrera 2025). These studies make reproductive microclimate and exposure plausible explanations for the observed spatial pattern. The present data, however, do not distinguish thermal regulation, precipitation exposure, developmental geometry or pollinator presentation. The image-derived angle is measured relative to EXIF-oriented image vertical rather than gravity, and the data are cross-sectional. The supported inference is therefore a robust orientation–temperature pattern and a set of mechanistic hypotheses for field testing, not demonstrated plasticity or adaptation.

## Visible colour occupies several environmental axes

Visible colour showed environmental structure across several analyses. In the frozen primary family, chroma increased with annual precipitation and circular hue components varied with temperature seasonality and precipitation structure. Grouped spatial models additionally associated lightness and chroma with temperature and soil pH, and among-taxon environmental separation was strongest for chroma and hue together with orientation. These results indicate that visible flower colour is not organized along one universal environmental axis.

The chroma–precipitation primary association survived collection-timing and dominant-taxon controls and retained its positive direction under native-range restriction, although the restricted-domain BH value rose to 0.0754. We therefore treat it as a supported but range-sensitive global association rather than as a result that disappears biologically when introduced-range observations are removed. Native-only analysis changes the domain being estimated and substantially reduces the observations and taxa available; loss of multiplicity support with a preserved direction is consequently evidence of weaker robustness, not evidence that the original global pattern never existed.

Image colour nevertheless requires especially careful interpretation because JPEG white balance, illumination and exposure can produce apparent gradients. A paired flower-versus-background negative control is therefore important before assigning corolla-specific optical or pigment mechanisms. Reviews and experiments nevertheless provide biologically plausible hypotheses involving temperature, drought, soil chemistry, herbivory and pollinators (Narbona et al. 2018; Vaidya et al. 2018; Borghi et al. 2019; Dalrymple et al. 2020; Sullivan & Koski 2021; Grossenbacher et al. 2026). In *Cirsium palustre*, purple, white and intermediate morph frequencies varied with exposure, population size and year, and pollination differed locally between morphs (Mogford 1972, 1974a,b, 1978). White-flowered *Cirsium* forms are also documented in East Asia (Kitamura 1932), while recent work on the Taiwanese *C. japonicum* complex shows that colour alone does not delimit evolutionary lineages (Chang et al. 2026). The global image results therefore motivate colour as a multicausal ecological axis without assigning a single pigment or pollinator mechanism.

## Outline shape contains selected, scale-dependent environmental structure

Outline traits were not uniformly associated with environment. Aspect ratio increased with temperature seasonality and decreased with annual precipitation in the global within-taxon analysis. Both associations retained direction after seasonal and dominant-taxon controls; the BIO4 association also retained multiplicity support under native-range restriction, whereas the BIO12 association did not. In contrast, grouped SPDE models provided little global BH support for gross-outline traits. At the among-taxon historical-sensitivity scale, solidity and width-profile variation appeared in retained randomized-tree associations.

These differences argue against treating capitulum outline as one ecological trait. They instead suggest that particular geometric dimensions contain environmental information at particular scales. This is precisely the advantage of replacing labels such as globose versus elongate with numerical shape variables: different components can be tested separately, and unsupported dimensions remain visible rather than being hidden inside a single category.

## Expanded involucral architecture reveals graded candidate signals

The first high-resolution contour screen provided a useful warning about measurement quality. Its three original BIO4 associations—projection roughness, outward spread fraction and maximum projection—did not satisfy the predeclared confirmatory image-quality rule after adjustment for resolution and sharpness, and two reversed sign in the lowest resolution stratum. Those rows should therefore remain legacy exploratory contour signals rather than evidence that botanical spine length or phyllary spreading is determined by temperature seasonality.

The expanded GEB v2 contract addressed that limitation by defining finer continuous image phenotypes before rerunning the candidate environment screen. Three candidate rows passed registry-wide FDR correction. Involucre length/width ratio increased with precipitation seasonality, apical taper decreased with annual precipitation, and upper-tail bract projection decreased with precipitation seasonality. Crucially, endpoint-specific quality sensitivity did not treat these three rows equally. The length/width and apical-taper signals retained their direction after resolution/sharpness adjustment and across all successful fixed resolution strata, whereas upper-tail projection showed resolution-stratum sign instability. The first two are therefore graded as quality-robust exploratory signals and the third as image-sensitive.

This distinction is the substantive result of the v2 candidate layer. It shows that finer measurement can recover continuous environmental structure that is not visible in coarse morphology categories, while the same workflow can identify which apparent signals remain vulnerable to image quality. The two quality-robust candidate associations are still C-grade, not confirmatory botanical effects: independent reference annotation is required to establish how closely the image-derived ratios correspond to specific phyllary or involucre structures in botanical terms.

Precipitation exposure and mechanical or defensive architecture provide plausible biological contexts, as do known links between spinescence and antagonists (Hanley et al. 2007; Lamp & McCarty 1982; Rose et al. 2005; Gijsman et al. 2020). But the present image metrics cannot establish stiffness, herbivore exclusion, developmental causation or adaptive function. Their present value is in mapping spatial phenotype–environment structure and generating sharper field hypotheses.

## Robustness analyses grade evidence rather than redefine the question

The primary estimand is the within-source-assigned-taxon spatial environment–trait association in the frozen global spatially thinned cohort. Sensitivity analyses address different possible distortions of that estimand. Cyclic date adjustment and dominant-taxon omission test sampling structure; hemisphere-aware timing checks phase definition; native-only restriction changes the geographic-status domain; image-resolution and sharpness analyses probe measurement quality directly. These controls should therefore not all be interpreted as identical pass/fail gates.

The final evidence hierarchy makes this explicit. Orientation–BIO1 and aspect ratio–BIO4 are A-grade because they retain support across the relevant primary sensitivities. Chroma–BIO12 and aspect ratio–BIO12 are B-grade range-sensitive patterns because their global directions persist while native-only multiplicity support weakens. Circular hue rows remain supported but colour-calibration dependent. In the expanded candidate tier, involucre length/width–BIO15 and apical taper–BIO12 are C-grade quality-robust signals, while upper-tail projection–BIO15 is C-grade image-sensitive. Candidate status is not promoted merely because an adjusted q value is small: botanical calibration and the predeclared tier boundary remain part of the evidence definition.

This evidence hierarchy preserves the distinction between discovering a broad spatial pattern and establishing the biological process responsible for it. It also avoids reducing a multivariate phenotypic survey to the handful of coefficients that survive every possible sensitivity threshold.

## Within-taxon and among-taxon views describe different parts of the same phenotypic landscape

Among-taxon environmental separation exceeded permutation expectations mainly for orientation and visible colour, while the combinations emphasized by within-taxon models were not identical. This mismatch is expected if geographic variation within taxa and turnover among taxa capture different components of phenotypic diversity. The two levels should therefore be read together: within-taxon models identify environmental alignment of repeated observations carrying the same taxon name, whereas among-taxon analyses ask whether taxa occupying different parts of morphospace also occupy different environmental spaces.

The phylogenetic layer remains a historical-placement sensitivity. Only 54 of 216 atlas taxa are direct dated-backbone tips, most taxa require within-genus placement and retained Pagel-λ estimates are zero. Coefficients that persist across randomized trees are insensitive to the tested placements, but this does not constitute a resolved species-tree correction. The appropriate inference is that some among-taxon environmental structure persists under the available historical scenarios, not that phylogeny has been definitively removed.

The broader history of *Cirsium* provides context for why such multiscale structure may be expected. The genus combines ecological diversity, rapid regional radiations, difficult generic boundaries and hybrid or polyploid origins (Kelch & Baldwin 2003; Ackerfield et al. 2020; Siniscalchi et al. 2023; Bureš et al. 2024; Moreyra et al. 2025; Chang et al. 2026). The present study does not test whether any measured capitulum trait caused diversification. It provides a quantitative global phenotype map that future evolutionary analyses can interrogate.

## Limits define interpretation, not the existence of pattern

Residual spatial autocorrelation was negligible, broad-region omission retained taxon rankings, niche permutations rejected quartile splitting as a complete explanation for the strongest among-taxon patterns and simultaneous synonym collapse left the primary results unchanged. Seasonal, dominant-taxon, hemisphere-aware and native-range analyses further show where individual associations are robust and where they are sensitive. Endpoint-specific resolution and sharpness analyses now do the same for the expanded candidate layer. These audits improve the description of the spatial pattern; they do not turn it into a causal ecological effect.

Several measurement limitations remain central. The detector was developed from pseudo-labels and requires adjudicated independent human boxes; continuous orientation, colour, outline and expanded candidate traits require independent reference measurements. Developmental stage is unlabelled, the paired colour negative control is pending and the repeat-photo cohort has not yet been remeasured. `visible_floret_fraction`, the only unexecuted inferential endpoint in the final GEB v2 run, cannot support a floral-display conclusion in this version. Public photographs also reflect geographic observer bias, uneven trait assessability, camera processing and viewpoint. The analysed geographic domain should therefore be described as the global public-image sample rather than a perfectly representative census of all *Cirsium* phenotype.

These limitations matter most when moving from pattern to mechanism. They are less destructive to the first-order goal of this paper: quantifying visible phenotype continuously, describing its spatial diversity and testing whether that diversity aligns with environmental gradients. Independent validation will determine how closely individual image metrics approximate botanical quantities; field experiments, common gardens, spectral measurements, interaction observations and fitness data would be required to establish plasticity, adaptation, pollinator selection or antagonist defence.

## Overall implication

The study positions public-image phenomics as a bridge between conventional trait databases and mechanistic field ecology. Trait databases provide broad comparative coverage but often rely on species means or categorical states; targeted field studies provide biological precision but usually cover fewer taxa and locations. Repeated citizen-science photographs occupy a complementary middle ground: they can turn globally distributed images into continuous, spatially explicit phenotype observations.

For thistles, that view reveals a multidimensional capitulum morphospace structured simultaneously within and among taxa and aligned with environment in trait- and scale-specific ways. Orientation and visible colour provide the most consistent established signals across analytical scales; selected outline dimensions show robust within-taxon structure; and the expanded continuous layer identifies two quality-robust exploratory involucral-geometry associations while correctly retaining a third as image-sensitive. The biological value of the analysis is therefore not a single climate coefficient or a single adaptive explanation. It is an evidence-graded quantitative global map of reproductive phenotype diversity that identifies which trait components vary, where they vary, which environmental axes align with that variation and which apparent patterns are sufficiently measurement-robust to motivate targeted botanical and mechanistic tests.
