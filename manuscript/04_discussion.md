# Discussion

## Repeated photographs change the unit of trait inference

Across every primary endpoint, at least 58.9% of visible image variance occurred below taxon means, and this conclusion persisted when one head was retained per photograph and replication was balanced among taxa. Taxon medians nevertheless formed several multivariate axes. Species- or taxon-level summaries therefore remain informative, but they are incomplete descriptions of capitulum phenotype. This result extends evidence that within-species variation can be a substantial component of trait variation (Messier et al. 2010; Albert et al. 2011; Siefert et al. 2015) to repeated numerical measurements extracted from global public photographs.

The below-taxon component combines biological heterogeneity with uncontrolled image conditions. It should not be interpreted as heritability, plasticity or genetic variance. One-head-per-photograph analysis removes head multiplicity but does not measure camera, illumination or viewpoint effects. Metadata show that 20,073 primary-cohort observations have at least two public photographs, providing a viable repeat-photo cohort, but those images have not yet been remeasured and may not all depict the same individual. The current decomposition therefore establishes repeated visible image variation, not the biological fraction of that variation.

## Orientation links environment to reproductive exposure

Orientation showed the clearest agreement across within-taxon approaches: head angle increased with annual mean temperature in both the primary and spatial models. Floral orientation can alter absorbed radiation, flower temperature, rain exposure, pollen and stigma timing, pollinator presentation and reproductive performance (Chen et al. 2013; van der Kooi et al. 2019; Creux et al. 2021). Cardueae capitula can also remain cooler than air under hot summer conditions (Herrera 2025). These observations make reproductive microclimate and exposure plausible mechanisms for the temperature association.

The image-derived angle, however, is measured relative to EXIF-oriented image vertical, not gravity, and the data are cross-sectional. The association persisted after raw-calendar and hemisphere-aware cyclic day-of-year adjustments, all predeclared dominant-taxon omissions and native-range restriction, reducing concerns about collection timing, concentration in widespread weeds and mixing native with introduced regions. Day-of-year is not an anthesis-stage label: camera position, plant posture and developmental stage can still contribute to the pattern. The result therefore identifies an orientation–temperature association and a field-testable mechanism, not thermal plasticity or an adaptation. Direct stage-labelled measurements of head angle, temperature, moisture and fitness across populations would be needed to distinguish those alternatives.

## Visible colour integrates several ecological axes

Uncalibrated image colour was associated with temperature, precipitation and soil pH in the frozen primary and grouped spatial families, but the directions differed among lightness, chroma and circular hue components. The primary chroma–precipitation row survived collection-timing and dominant-taxon controls but failed the separately frozen native-only BH rule (q = 0.0754) and is therefore not a current biological headline result. JPEG white balance and illumination remain capable of producing image-colour gradients; paired flower-versus-background `region × BIO12` tests are still required before any broader corolla-specific precipitation interpretation. Experimental and comparative studies nevertheless provide biological hypotheses involving temperature, drought, soil chemistry, herbivory and pollinators (Narbona et al. 2018; Vaidya et al. 2018; Borghi et al. 2019; Dalrymple et al. 2020; Sullivan & Koski 2021; Grossenbacher et al. 2026).

Within *Cirsium palustre*, purple, white and intermediate morph frequencies varied with exposure, population size and year, and pollination differed locally between morphs (Mogford 1972, 1974a,b, 1978). White-flowered *Cirsium* forms have also long been documented in East Asia (Kitamura 1932), yet recent work on the Taiwanese *C. japonicum* complex shows that white and bluish-purple corollas can occur within a named variety and that colour alone does not delimit lineages (Chang et al. 2026). These studies support taking colour variation seriously while warning against assigning one mechanism or taxonomic meaning to it. Our hue sine and cosine coefficients likewise describe one circular response jointly; neither component alone defines a biological colour shift.

## Involucral architecture remains unresolved

The original positive BIO4 coefficients for projection roughness, outward spread fraction and maximum spine-like projection did not meet the predeclared image-resolution retention rule. Their adjusted full-cohort q values were 0.0696–0.0730, and two endpoints reversed sign in the lowest resolution stratum. Outward spread fraction was the exception in sign stability: it remained positive in all three resolution strata, but its adjusted q = 0.0696 still failed the frozen rule. This is a sampling-design clue for a resolution-balanced follow-up, not a retained result. Fine involucral architecture is therefore not a supported environmental result in the current claim set.

Spinescence and seed predation remain relevant hypotheses for independent botanical work (Hanley et al. 2007; Lamp & McCarty 1982; Rose et al. 2005; Gijsman et al. 2020), but they cannot rescue a row that failed its image-quality control. The two-dimensional proxies also cannot distinguish longer bract tips from spreading or recurvature and do not measure stiffness or herbivore exclusion. Any future test requires direct phyllary measurements and adequate resolution strata.

## Capitulum environmental structure is modular and scale dependent

The results do not define a single warm-, wet- or dry-climate thistle morphology. Orientation retained an annual-mean-temperature association and aspect ratio retained a temperature-seasonality association through the native-range audit. Visible-colour structure remained evident in the frozen spatial and among-taxon families, but the primary chroma–precipitation row did not pass native-only BH correction. Most gross-outline traits lacked spatial-model support, and the involucral layer did not survive its image-quality audit. A conspicuous taxonomic difference need not be the trait most closely associated with environment, and a large variance component need not imply a strong climate coefficient.

Within- and among-taxon patterns were also not interchangeable. Within taxa, annual mean temperature was most consistent for orientation, whereas the primary precipitation–chroma row was withdrawn after native-range restriction and still awaits its imaging control. Among taxa, environmental separation exceeded permutation expectations mainly for orientation and visible colour, but direct-backbone support narrowed. The PGLS layer is presented in Supporting Information because Pagel-λ was zero, only 54 of 216 taxa were direct backbone tips and no non-circular direct-backbone signal survived FDR correction. Such scale differences remain descriptive rather than resolved historical inference (Messier et al. 2010; Albert et al. 2011; Violle et al. 2012).

The broader history of *Cirsium* provides context, not proof of mechanism. The genus combines ecological diversity, rapid regional radiations, difficult generic boundaries and hybrid or polyploid origins (Kelch & Baldwin 2003; Ackerfield et al. 2020; Siniscalchi et al. 2023; Bureš et al. 2024; Moreyra et al. 2025; Chang et al. 2026). Our data do not test whether capitulum modules caused diversification. They show that visible capitulum diversity is environmentally structured in trait- and scale-specific ways that future evolutionary analyses can test.

## Robustness and remaining limitations

Residual spatial autocorrelation was negligible, broad-region omission retained taxon rankings, niche permutations rejected quartile splitting as a complete explanation for the strongest among-taxon patterns, and simultaneous synonym collapse left the primary results unchanged. The fail-closed WCVP/TDWG native-only analysis then narrowed the four non-circular primary rows to two. These checks reduce concerns about residual spatial structure, single-region dominance, the permutation design, operational taxon names and introduced-range mixing. They do not turn observational coefficients into causal ecological effects.

Four measurement limitations remain central. The detector was developed from pseudo-labels and requires adjudicated independent human boxes; continuous orientation, colour and outline require independent reference measurements. Developmental stage is unlabelled, the visible-colour negative control is unexecuted, and the repeat-photo cohort has not yet been measured. Internal development metrics, quality-control retention and mirror repeatability cannot establish biological accuracy. Public photographs additionally reflect geographic observer bias, uneven trait assessability, camera processing and viewpoint. Geographic density and assessability therefore describe the analysed image domain, not representative global sampling of all *Cirsium*. The native-range join resolved 245 of 259 source taxa and classified 58.5% of observations as native; 5,491 observations from 14 ambiguously resolved taxa, including all records assigned to *C. palustre*, were excluded fail-closed. Native-only estimates therefore remain a sensitivity of the authority-resolved image domain, not a reconstruction of introduction history.

Phylogenetic inference is similarly bounded. Only 54 of 216 atlas taxa were direct dated-backbone tips, and most taxa required within-genus placement. Coefficients that persisted across randomized trees are insensitive to the tested placements, but the trees do not resolve thistle history. The appropriate interpretation is evidence for some among-taxon environmental structure, not definitive removal of shared ancestry.

## Implications

Taxon means do not capture all visible capitulum variation, and the two retained primary climate rows are not driven solely by cyclic collection timing, the most sampled taxa or introduced-range observations. The contribution is therefore a disciplined route from public images to repeated quantitative phenotype hypotheses, together with explicit failure and hold rules. Biological interpretation remains conditional on developmental-stage, colour-specificity, repeat-photo and independent validation gates.
