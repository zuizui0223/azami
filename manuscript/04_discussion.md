# Discussion

## From trait categories to a present phenotypic field

The main contribution of this study is a change in the unit of broad-scale trait description. Rather than representing each thistle taxon by one categorical state or one mean value, public photographs were converted into repeated continuous measurements of visible capitulum phenotype. Across every established primary endpoint, at least 58.9% of visible image variance occurred below taxon means, and this conclusion persisted when one head was retained per photograph and replication was balanced among taxa. Taxon medians remained informative, but they were incomplete summaries of the phenotype visible across geography.

The GEB v2 expansion makes that conclusion more stringent. Eighteen inferential endpoints were measured across orientation, visible colour, outline, involucre architecture and armature. In the 127-taxon complete morphospace, the first three principal components explained only 42.3% of variance. Finer measurement therefore did not reveal one hidden master axis. It reconstructed a multidimensional present phenotypic field in which several components of one reproductive structure vary partly independently.

This extends the argument that intraspecific variation is a substantial component of ecological trait variation (Messier et al. 2010; Albert et al. 2011; Bolnick et al. 2011; Violle et al. 2012; Siefert et al. 2015) from conventional measurements to repeated numerical phenotypes extracted from global public imagery. The claim is not that public photographs provide error-free biological measurements. It is that, when assessability and measurement scope are explicit, repeated images preserve phenotype distributions that coarse states and taxon means compress away.

The below-taxon component still combines biological heterogeneity with uncontrolled image conditions. It must not be interpreted as heritability, plasticity or genetic variance. One-head-per-photograph analysis removes head multiplicity but not camera, illumination, viewpoint or developmental-stage effects. The frozen repeat-photo cohort provides a route to estimate part of that observation-layer contribution, but it has not yet been remeasured. Thus, Chapter 1 establishes a repeated visible phenotype field and the information lost through taxon averaging; it does not yet partition all biological and photographic sources of variance.

## A capitulum is partially organized, not one syndrome and not a bag of independent traits

Returning from separate endpoint coefficients to the whole capitulum was necessary because Asteraceae capitula are flower-like reproductive units whose component structures can contribute to attraction, presentation, protection and reproductive geometry (Baczyński & Claßen-Bockhoff 2023; Zhang & Elomaa 2024). The expanded analysis supports a middle position between two oversimplifications.

First, the head is not one universal syndrome. The 18-endpoint morphospace is high dimensional, and the environmental results do not collapse onto one warm-, wet- or dry-climate morphology. Second, the endpoints are not independent measurements with no internal organization. Association strength was higher within registered measurement modules than between modules both within and among taxa.

The key result is the difference between scales. The module contrast was 0.1645 within taxa but only 0.0885 among taxa, while the full within- and among-taxon association matrices had a Spearman similarity of 0.3663. The phenotype therefore contains shared organization, but within-taxon covariance is not merely a small-scale version of among-taxon covariance. This is consistent with floral-integration theory in which different subsets of reproductive structures can be partially integrated (Diggle 2014), but the present evidence remains phenotypic and measurement based. Shared segmentation, viewpoint and image processing may contribute to module covariance. The data do not establish functional, developmental or genetic modularity.

This distinction changes the meaning of “functionally annotated trait space.” Literature can identify plausible roles for orientation, colour, outline and involucre architecture, but it cannot retroactively prove that the registered image modules are functional modules. The defensible result is a partially organized phenotype space whose functional interpretation remains a downstream hypothesis.

## Environmental organization changes with biological scale

The matched analysis provides the clearest reason to treat within- and among-taxon structure as different biological questions. Across 68 endpoint × core-climate rows, only three were supported at both scales, eight were within-taxon only, one was among-taxon only and 56 were supported at neither scale. Orientation aligned with annual mean temperature within taxa but annual precipitation among taxa. The three final candidate involucral rows were all within-taxon only.

These results rule out a simple scale-invariance story. A within-taxon spatial gradient cannot automatically be interpreted as the process that generated taxon turnover, and an among-taxon association cannot be read backwards as local plasticity. Within-taxon models describe how repeated observations carrying the same source-assigned taxon name vary across space. Among-taxon models describe whether taxa occupying different phenotype regions also occupy different environmental regions. The two levels are connected, but they are not interchangeable.

This is stronger than saying that effect sizes differ. The organization of the phenotype–environment relationship itself changes with scale. The partial matrix similarity shows that some trait relationships persist, whereas the matched endpoint classifications show that many environmental alignments do not. The Chapter 1 conclusion is therefore scale-dependent environmental organization, not a universal climate-associated thistle morphology.

## The sufficiency of the environmental representation is itself scale dependent

The four established CHELSA variables—annual mean temperature, temperature seasonality, annual precipitation and precipitation seasonality—remain a useful low-dimensional core. The process extension asked a more precise question than whether additional variables were individually significant: do radiation, VPD, wind, growing-season precipitation and potential NPP contain multivariate information not represented by the core four?

Within taxa, the answer was no for the current 18D estimand. The omnibus partial R² was 0.0135 and unsupported, and no block-specific extension survived multiplicity control. This supports retaining the four-variable core for the within-taxon Chapter 1 analysis rather than reopening a large correlated-raster screen.

Among taxa, the answer was different. The five-variable extension increased R² from 0.0797 to 0.2775 in the main scope, with partial R² 0.2150 and permutation P = 0.000700. Growing-season precipitation was the only block-specific increment supported at both replication thresholds. Radiation plus VPD and potential NPP were supported only in the stricter replication scope, and wind was unsupported.

This does not demonstrate a direct growing-season precipitation mechanism. The added variable may represent phenological water input, correlated climatic geography, vegetation context or historical taxon turnover. The robust inference is narrower: the four core variables do not exhaust the environmental information associated with among-taxon phenotype structure. Thus, even the dimensionality required to describe environment depends on the biological scale of the response.

The standardized coefficient-vector cosines do not support a stronger directional claim. Their uncertainty intervals include zero, so they remain descriptive geometry. The scale dependence is established by the matched endpoint pattern, the within/among covariance comparison and the nested environment-sufficiency test, not by a claim of opposing selection or stable vector rotation.

## Orientation remains the strongest established endpoint-level pattern

Orientation showed the clearest agreement across within-taxon approaches. Image-referenced head angle increased with annual mean temperature in the primary and grouped spatial analyses, and its direction persisted after raw-calendar and hemisphere-aware cyclic adjustment, dominant-taxon omission and native-range restriction. This makes orientation–BIO1 one of the strongest endpoint-level environmental patterns in the study.

Floral orientation can alter absorbed radiation, flower temperature, rain exposure, pollen and stigma timing, pollinator presentation and reproductive performance (Chen et al. 2013; van der Kooi et al. 2019; Creux et al. 2021). Cardueae capitula can also remain cooler than air under hot summer conditions (Herrera 2025). These findings make reproductive microclimate and exposure plausible candidate mechanisms. The global association does not distinguish among thermal regulation, precipitation exposure, developmental geometry or pollinator presentation. The angle is measured relative to EXIF-oriented image vertical rather than gravity, and the observations are cross sectional. The supported inference remains a robust spatial orientation pattern, not demonstrated plasticity or adaptation.

Its among-taxon association with annual precipitation further illustrates why mechanism cannot be assigned from one scale. The same image endpoint is aligned with different climatic axes within and among taxa. A mechanistic explanation must therefore reproduce both the local-scale pattern and the taxon-turnover pattern rather than selecting one coefficient in isolation.

## Visible colour is a multicausal and calibration-limited axis

Visible colour showed environmental organization across several analyses. Chroma increased with annual precipitation in the frozen primary family, hue components varied with temperature and precipitation structure, grouped spatial models associated lightness and chroma with temperature and soil pH, and among-taxon sorting was concentrated in colour together with orientation. These patterns argue against one universal colour–environment axis.

The chroma–precipitation association survived collection-timing and dominant-taxon controls and retained its positive direction under native-range restriction, although the restricted-domain BH value rose to 0.0754. It is therefore a supported but range-sensitive global association, not an A-grade native-range-robust result.

JPEG white balance, illumination and exposure can produce apparent colour gradients. A paired flower-versus-background control remains necessary before assigning corolla-specific optical or pigment mechanisms. Reviews and experiments nevertheless identify plausible hypotheses involving temperature, drought, soil chemistry, herbivory and pollinators (Narbona et al. 2018; Vaidya et al. 2018; Borghi et al. 2019; Dalrymple et al. 2020; Sullivan & Koski 2021; Grossenbacher et al. 2026). In *Cirsium palustre*, colour-morph frequencies varied with exposure, population size and year, and pollination differed locally between morphs (Mogford 1972, 1974a,b, 1978). White-flowered *Cirsium* forms occur in East Asia (Kitamura 1932), while recent work on the Taiwanese *C. japonicum* complex shows that colour alone does not delimit evolutionary lineages (Chang et al. 2026). The present images therefore motivate colour as a multicausal axis without identifying one pigment or pollinator mechanism.

## Outline and involucral geometry show selective rather than universal structure

Outline traits were not uniformly associated with environment. Aspect ratio increased with temperature seasonality and decreased with annual precipitation in the global within-taxon analysis. Both retained direction under seasonal and dominant-taxon controls, while only the BIO4 association retained native-only multiplicity support. Grouped SPDE models provided little global BH support for gross-outline traits. Numerical decomposition therefore exposes which geometric dimensions contain environmental information instead of hiding supported and unsupported components inside labels such as globose or elongate.

The involucral analyses also show why measurement resolution and evidence grading matter. The original three-proxy contour screen failed the predeclared image-quality confirmation, and two rows reversed sign in the lowest-resolution stratum. Those results were withdrawn from the biological headline rather than reinterpreted after the fact.

The expanded contract produced three registry-wide candidate signals. Involucre length/width–BIO15 and apical taper–BIO12 retained direction after resolution/sharpness adjustment and across fixed resolution strata; `bract_projection_p95`–BIO15 did not. The first two are therefore quality-robust C-grade image-phenotype associations, and the third is image-sensitive. None passed matched among-taxon FDR.

Precipitation exposure, reproductive shielding, mechanical architecture and antagonist defence are plausible contexts, as are known links between spinescence and antagonists (Hanley et al. 2007; Lamp & McCarty 1982; Rose et al. 2005; Gijsman et al. 2020). The image metrics do not measure stiffness, actual spine length, herbivore exclusion or adaptive function. Their value is that they sharpen the next botanical and field hypotheses while preserving a lower evidence grade.

## Robustness analyses define evidence strength rather than one universal veto

The primary estimand is the within-source-assigned-taxon spatial environment–trait association in the frozen global cohort. The sensitivity analyses address different distortions of that estimand. Cyclic date adjustment and dominant-taxon omission test sampling structure; hemisphere-aware timing tests phase definition; native-only restriction changes the geographic-status domain; spatial and region-omission analyses address geographic dependence; synonym collapse addresses operational taxonomy; resolution and sharpness probe candidate measurement quality. These are not interchangeable pass/fail gates.

The final hierarchy makes this explicit. Orientation–BIO1 and aspect ratio–BIO4 are A-grade. Chroma–BIO12 and aspect ratio–BIO12 are B-grade range-sensitive patterns. Circular hue rows remain calibration limited. In the candidate tier, length/width–BIO15 and apical taper–BIO12 are C-grade quality-robust, while `bract_projection_p95`–BIO15 is C-grade image-sensitive. The whole-capitulum organization and environment-sufficiency analyses form a separate multivariate layer and do not promote the endpoint-level candidate rows.

Residual Moran's I was negligible, broad-region omission retained taxon rankings, synonym collapse left the primary results effectively unchanged and environmental-sorting permutations constrained the strongest among-taxon patterns. These audits strengthen the description of the current phenotype field. They do not turn spatial association into causal effect.

## The unresolved limits are measurement and history, not another correlational screen

Several limitations remain submission blocking. The detector requires adjudicated independent human boxes; orientation, colour and outline require genuinely independent reference measurements; candidate involucre and armature metrics require botanical calibration; developmental stage is unlabelled; the paired colour control is pending; and repeat photographs have not been remeasured. `visible_floret_fraction` was not executed, so Chapter 1 cannot make a floral-display conclusion from that endpoint.

The phylogenetic layer also remains limited. Only 54 of 216 atlas taxa are direct dated-backbone tips, most taxa require within-genus placement and retained Pagel λ estimates are zero. Coefficients that persist across randomized placements are useful historical sensitivities, but they do not constitute resolved species-tree correction. The broader history of *Cirsium*—including regional radiations, difficult boundaries, hybridization and polyploid origins—explains why a resolved history is important (Kelch & Baldwin 2003; Ackerfield et al. 2020; Siniscalchi et al. 2023; Bureš et al. 2024; Moreyra et al. 2025; Chang et al. 2026), but Chapter 1 does not infer that any measured trait caused diversification.

These gaps cannot be solved by adding more uncontracted climate rasters. They require independent measurement, repeat-image controls, developmental information, field experiments, interaction and fitness data, and better historical resolution.

## From the phenotypic present to generative history

Azami and EAzami are best understood as one directed research line. Azami reconstructs the present phenotypic field:

`continuous traits → repeated distributions → spatial hierarchy → frozen within/among phenotype–environment geometry`.

EAzami begins from that frozen field and asks which declared histories and mechanism families can generate it:

`candidate function → interaction and fitness structure → historical models → admissible or rejected generators → next experiment`.

This boundary prevents two opposite errors. Azami does not overinterpret observational associations as mechanism, and EAzami does not replace the measured present with a preferred historical story. A successful generator would demonstrate structural sufficiency under declared assumptions, not causal proof. A failed generator is also informative because it identifies which aspects of the present field—such as moderate within-taxon organization, weaker among-taxon integration and stronger among-taxon process structure—remain unexplained.

The downstream programme should therefore treat the 62 machine-readable Azami targets as constraints on generative history, not as independent causal effects. Functional literature and future field measurements can then discriminate among candidate explanations without changing the Chapter 1 observation layer.

## Overall implication

Public-image phenomics occupies a useful middle ground between broad trait databases and targeted field ecology. Trait databases provide comparative coverage but often compress taxa into means or states; field studies provide functional precision but usually cover fewer taxa and locations. Repeated biodiversity photographs can retain continuous, spatially explicit phenotype distributions across a large geographic domain.

For thistles, that view reveals extensive visible diversity below taxon means, a high-dimensional taxon morphospace, partial organization of one reproductive structure, incomplete correspondence between within- and among-taxon covariance, endpoint-level associations that differ by trait and scale, and a process-environment representation required among taxa but not within taxa. The contribution is therefore not one climate coefficient or one adaptive explanation. It is a quantitative reconstruction of the present phenotypic field and a frozen set of patterns that future functional and evolutionary analyses must jointly explain.
