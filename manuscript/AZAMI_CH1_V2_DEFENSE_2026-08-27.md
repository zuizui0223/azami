# Azami Chapter 1 v2: method, ecological result and defence boundary

## Frozen contribution

Azami Chapter 1 v2 establishes a reproducible route from public biodiversity photographs to a continuous, coordinate-bearing phenotype atlas. A detector localizes visible capitula, after which deterministic image functions quantify orientation, visible corolla colour, outline and assessable involucral or armature-like geometry. The workflow begins from 27 registered endpoints, reports 22 measured endpoints and preserves five unexecuted endpoints as missing evidence rather than biological absence. It retains repeated observations instead of replacing a taxon by one category or one mean.

This change of unit is the primary methodological result. Among 46,276 spatially thinned observations from 259 source-assigned taxa, 81.4-98.6% of raw endpoint variation occurred below taxon means. After equalizing replication to two observations per eligible taxon, endpoint medians still ranged from 28.7% to 58.5%. Because photographs carry coordinates, the same continuous definitions support trait-specific geography, six predeclared environmental blocks and matched within- versus among-taxon analyses. The contribution is therefore not merely automated scoring: it is an auditable method for recovering repeated, spatially explicit phenotype distributions that categorical and taxon-mean databases compress.

## Ecological conclusion

The present phenotype field is trait- and scale-specific rather than one whole-capitulum syndrome. Across the canonical 26 inferential units by nine environmental predictors, the primary comparison contained seven both-scale, 18 within-only, three among-only, 152 neither and 54 not-comparable rows. The complete atlas remains visible; robustness gates annotate rather than delete unsupported or fragile results.

Two among-taxon rows are the final adaptive-pattern candidates under current controls:

1. Taxa from higher-shortwave-radiation environments had lower camera-recorded CIELAB corolla chroma.
2. Taxa from higher-annual-precipitation environments had a larger head-axis angle on the frozen image scale, where 0 degrees points upward, 90 degrees is horizontal and 180 degrees points downward relative to EXIF-oriented image vertical.

Both rows passed their global multiplicity family, the broad spherical-coordinate trend test and the residual-spatial-autocorrelation condition. Both retained direction in every one of 18 applicable sampling-composition scenarios and retained direction and P < 0.05 on all 52 historical-placement trees. The public-image sample is nevertheless geographically and taxonomically uneven, historical placements are not a fully resolved species tree, colour is not spectrally calibrated and image vertical is not a gravity measurement.

## Candidate 1: chroma and radiation

The defensible observation is **lower visible CIELAB chroma among taxa occurring under higher mean shortwave radiation**. Experimental and geographic studies outside *Cirsium* show that floral anthocyanin production can respond to radiation: UV-B induced petal anthocyanin accumulation in rose, and *Clarkia unguiculata* populations originating under higher solar UV had higher estimated floral anthocyanin concentration (Hennayake et al. 2006; Peach et al. 2020). These results make radiation-responsive pigment regulation, including anthocyanin synthesis, a biologically grounded candidate mechanism.

The sign is not itself a pigment assay. CIELAB chroma is the radial distance in visible a-star/b-star colour space, not anthocyanin concentration, UV reflectance or pathway expression. Increased pigment loading can interact with lightness, hue, copigmentation, petal optics, senescence and camera processing; therefore lower photographed chroma cannot by itself be called increased anthocyanin synthesis. The v2 defence is that the spatially and historically stable radiation-colour pattern is **compatible with a radiation-responsive floral-pigment hypothesis that now has a precise test**, not that anthocyanin mediation has been demonstrated. The decisive next test is calibrated visible/UV reflectance plus direct petal anthocyanin chemistry or pathway expression under common-garden radiation manipulation.

## Candidate 2: orientation and annual precipitation

The defensible observation is **a larger image-referenced head-axis angle among taxa occurring under higher annual precipitation**. On the frozen 0-up/90-horizontal/180-down image scale, this is consistent with a shift toward more downward-facing presentation in photographed orientation. Manipulation of the nodding Asteraceae *Cremanthodium campanulatum* showed that water and UV-B reduced pollen viability and artificially erect capitula produced fewer achenes than naturally nodding capitula, supporting rain and reproductive-tissue shielding as a plausible functional family (Chen et al. 2013).

Azami does not measure gravitational inclination, rainfall during anthesis, water reaching pollen or stigma, or reproductive fitness. The v2 defence is therefore that rain shielding is a taxonomically relevant and experimentally grounded hypothesis consistent with the observed direction, not the established cause of the global coefficient. The decisive next test is gravity-referenced three-dimensional head orientation combined with flowering-period rain exposure, pollen retention/viability and seed output, ideally including experimental angle manipulation.

## Bias and claim defence

- Sampling composition: all 674 declared scenarios were evaluable; 16 of 26 selected within-taxon and all ten selected among-taxon directions were stable throughout. Both final candidates were stable in all 18 applicable scenarios.
- Spatial structure: candidate passage required a broad spherical-coordinate trend, preserved direction and no detectable residual eight-nearest-neighbour Moran signal.
- Historical dependence: both candidates retained direction and support across all 52 declared placement trees, while direct-backbone scarcity and grafting remain explicit limitations.
- Measurement: failed traits remain missing, circular hue is tested jointly, colour calibration and independent orientation reference measurements remain submission-blocking gates.
- Claim ceiling: the candidates are stable present-geography targets for functional validation. They are not proof of plasticity, heritability, selection, adaptation, anthocyanin mediation, rain-protection function, independent origin or convergence.

## Frozen handoff

EAzami receives each continuous definition, uncertainty, present distribution, geographic support, environmental association, within/among scale class and measurement/q/sampling-composition/spatial/historical status. EAzami-I tests function, EAzami-II reconstructs repeated history, EAzami-III distinguishes retention, independent origin, ancestral polymorphism and introgression, and EAzami-IV tests convergence or adaptive convergence only after common selection pressure and fitness evidence are available.
