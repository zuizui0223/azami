# Part 2: native trait–environment correspondence and phenotypic redistribution

Question fixed 2026-11-27: 在来域で長期的に形成されてきた形質―環境関係は、新規環境へ進出した外来域でも維持されるのか。それとも、既存の種内変異が異なる形で選別されるのか。

The data compare present-day observations; duration of formation, inheritance, adaptive fitness, allele-frequency change and the actual introduction source are not measured. Maintenance of average association and redistribution of existing phenotypes are not mutually exclusive.

## Fixed comparison
1. The existing annual continuous analysis estimates within-range partial linear climate slopes and their introduced-minus-native contrasts, for the same three traits and five annual CHELSA variables as Part 1 (BIO12/BIO01/BIO04/BIO15/RSDS). Distinguish sign agreement, difference tests and equivalence; a nonsignificant difference does not establish maintenance. Full ranges and symmetric five-axis common support are reported separately, 1 degree primary and 5 degrees sensitivity. No warm-quarter, flowering-season, rarity or elevation predictors.
2. To assess phenotype redistribution even if the mean slopes agree, compare the actual native and introduced distributions in one-to-one climate-matched 1-degree cells. Standardize the five axes using equal native cells; match maximum cardinality then minimum RMS distance with the previously fixed caliper (RMS <= .9/sqrt(3), maximum absolute per-axis difference <= 1 native SD). Do not use traits for matching, or change cutoffs based on results. Geographic blocks need not be the same across native and introduced ranges; this does not eliminate introduction source or regional confounding. Show all-axis balance, common support and matched/unmatched counts before interpreting. No phenotype truncation, bins or core/outside-core climate categories in this comparison. All valid continuous observation values are retained.
3. In matched pairs fit a pair-intercept pseudo-logistic density-ratio model with continuous standardized trait values (native median and native SD) and their squared terms, all three traits simultaneously. Each cell contributes total weight one. Report conditional relative density curves with the native median as reference, plus empirical means, SDs and quantiles. Positive/negative curvature and direction describe observed redistribution, not natural selection. Require 25 pairs and >=8 blocks in each range. Use profiled pair scores with shared-geographic-endpoint (dyadic) cluster sandwich covariance, conservative min(region counts)-1 t degrees of freedom, Holm12 (two species x three traits x two terms); covariance failures remain descriptive, never forced to zero. This is exploratory inference conditional on the fixed matched sample, not an independent replication.
4. Preserve original core95/density-surplus niche comparisons and B curves as distinct descriptive questions about occupancy outside the native core. In climates with no adequate native counterpart, a native-vs-introduced conditional contrast is not empirically identifiable; do not extrapolate the native regression as proof of retention or change. The old native-prediction failure remains visible. No new distance/rarity predictor or thresholds chosen to inflate support.

Part 1 population: taxa-level correspondence. Part 2: within-species, two focal taxa; same climate variables but different statistical estimands. Report mean-slope results and conditional distributions together. Neither an environmental association nor a phenotypic density ratio establishes genetic adaptation. Preserve the original records and main branch.
