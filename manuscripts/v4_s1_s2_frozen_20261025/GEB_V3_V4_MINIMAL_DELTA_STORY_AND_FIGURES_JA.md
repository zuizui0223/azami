## Complete biological story, not a novelty checklist

**Question:** An image of a capitulum contains several continuous dimensions: presentation, visible colour and head outline. How are those traits environmentally organised across native taxa, across historically distinct native and introduced parts of the same species, and across covariance domains within the same photographed heads?

**Methodological axis — how the question becomes measurable:** Public biodiversity photos supply repeated georeferenced numeric phenotypes instead of one categorical state per species. Explicit per-trait missingness, image-frame angle rather than gravity angle, CIELAB image colour rather than pigment chemistry, observer balancing, source provenance and within-/among-taxon distinctions define what is actually estimated. This V3 motivation is preserved, not rebranded as a fresh scientific claim.

**Ecological axis — what the V4 observations say:**
- **Part 1:** higher warm-quarter BIO18 corresponds to larger *image-frame* presentation angles **among native taxa** (Holm P=.009206); a macroecological pattern worth independent functional testing, not evidence of rain protection.
- **Part 2:** the two focal species were selected for both historically informative native–introduced ranges and independent photo/site/observer support. Three photographed colour gradients in introduced strata survive Holm: *vulgare* **higher chroma (+.1345) and lower lightness (−.1428) under greater solar RSDS**; *arvense* **lower lightness (−.1737) at higher elevation**. The positive *vulgare* radiation–chroma association is **qualitatively consistent** with Dalrymple et al. (2020)'s prediction/observation of increased chromatic contrast under stronger radiation; elevational floral colour gradients in other species (Berardi et al. 2016) provide context for *arvense*, **not a direct colour-pigment validation**. Native *vulgare* chroma×RSDS β+.1369 is similar (BH P=.01776 but Holm=.1024); *arvense* native lightness×elevation β+.0563 is opposite signed. Those are original separate within-stratum fits, not formal differences.
- **Part 3:** within the same V4 native-photo sample, colour/outline show stronger within-domain photographic RV and multiple visible trait combinations coexist; the photo phenotype is neither an immutable all-traits package nor demonstrated freely recombinable heritable modules. Coding and shared pixels can explain part of the observed structure.

**Synthesis:** V4 provides both (a) a tractable quantitative way to observe multidimensional floral phenotype and (b) several environmentally interpretable, **trait-, scale- and range-specific** associations. Phenotypic integration gives these separate climatic results a coherent whole-capitulum context. Avoid claims that photos reveal selection/anthocyanin, that an introduced-only Holm threshold is a proven introduced effect, or that module covariance is genetic modularity.
