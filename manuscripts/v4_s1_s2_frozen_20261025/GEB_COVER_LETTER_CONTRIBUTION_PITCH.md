# Working contribution paragraph for a future GEB cover letter

**Status:** editorial draft only; no submission, correspondence, author identity, priority claim or journal acceptance implied. This contribution statement is deliberately **separate from the scientific Results/Discussion**.

> We would like to submit a Research Article on global photographic phenomics of *Cirsium* capitula to *Global Ecology and Biogeography*. Existing trait databases are indispensable, but categorical descriptions and taxon means compress the distribution and combinations of floral phenotypes observed across climates. By quantifying head presentation, corolla colour and outline geometry in repeated public photographs, we address three related ecological questions using source-verified, scale-aware comparisons. First, 168,907 native angle-eligible records across 178 taxa revealed a positive association of warm-quarter precipitation with image-referenced head presentation after the original Holm correction; spatial-model sensitivity is reported separately without asserting climate–geography causal separation. Second, source-audited within-species comparisons across native and introduced ranges identified three Holm-supported introduced-range floral colour gradients with solar radiation or elevation, interpreted alongside the corresponding native coefficients rather than as adaptation caused by introduction. Third, a new 3D analysis of 63 sufficiently photographed taxa tested whether broader realised climatic occupancy predicts a wider floral morphospace and whether climatically similar cross-region taxa occupy similar floral forms. Neither exploratory relationship was clearly supported, motivating a distinction between ecological niche breadth and observable morphological diversity rather than claiming morphological equivalence or independent evolutionary convergence. This combination of global image-derived phenotypes, explicit spatial sampling opportunity and historically bounded ecological inference addresses questions central to macroecology and biogeography.
