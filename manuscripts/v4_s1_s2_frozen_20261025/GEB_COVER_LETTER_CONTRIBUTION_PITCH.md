# Working contribution paragraph for a future GEB cover letter

**Status:** editorial draft only; no submission, correspondence, author identity, priority claim or journal acceptance implied. This contribution statement is deliberately **separate from the scientific Results/Discussion**.

> We would like to submit our Research Article on global photographic phenomics of *Cirsium* capitula to *Global Ecology and Biogeography*. Categorical flower descriptions and single species means compress within- and among-taxon variation across climates, leaving questions about geographic climatic organisation poorly resolved. We quantify head orientation, corolla colour and outline shape from repeated global public photographs. First, 168,907 angle-eligible native observations across 178 taxa reveal a positive warm-quarter precipitation–image-angle relationship in the original 80-test Holm family. Second, native and introduced ranges of two widespread species show three Holm-supported floral colour associations with radiation or elevation, without demonstrating rapid adaptation. Third, using 157,822 matched photographic observations, we show a new **local climatic-analogue correspondence**: taxa from different geographical sectors display significantly closer three-trait head phenotypes at actual sites with matching precipitation, temperature and radiation than expected after within-taxon geographic reassignment. This signal repeats under 1° and 5° cell sampling (exploratory Holm P=0.010) and has a corresponding fixed-kernel density-overlap pattern relative to the predeclared null, although the species-level climatic niche breadth–floral morphospace volume association is unresolved. We keep spatial-model dependence and nonsignificant climate×trait-covariance contrasts in Supporting Information. The study demonstrates how global public-image phenomics detects conditional ecological patterns missed by species-wide means without conflating photographic trait resemblance with adaptive evolutionary convergence.

## New source-confirmed H3c/H3d endpoints (29 October 2026)

**H3c — positive ecological pattern:** 157,822 common valid photographs/242 source taxa; **63 geographically supported taxa at 1°** and 31 at 5°. For different-sector taxa, matching the three-dimensional climate conditions of occupied cells (BIO12/BIO01/RSDS) yields closer three-dimensional photographed capitulum phenotypes (angle/chroma/aspect) than expected under within-taxon climate–trait permutation: **distance advantage +0.04096, Holm-2 P=0.010** (1°; mean 229 supported cross-sector pairs) and **+0.09433, Holm-2 P=0.010** (5°; mean 103 pairs). A fixed-bandwidth conditional 3D KDE L2 overlap comparison also deviated from its taxon-preserving null (Holm-2 P 0.010 / 0.015). **The conditional subset's absolute overlap was LOWER than the full eight-cell overlap**; this is a relative-to-permutation result, not an absolute increase in niche intersection. The H3a species-level breadth slope +0.0669 (CI −0.1046,+0.2384) and H3b species centroid ρ−0.0081 are not supported.

**H3d — not a positive claim:** Three trait-specific climatic-breadth responses and six rainfall/radiation-modulated cross-trait covariance contrasts were prespecified and tested on the same matched source; none survived their respective exploratory Holm families. In particular, the nominal 1° solar-radiation × image-angle–outline covariance P=0.016 had Holm-6 P=0.096 and a non-replicating/opposite-signed 5° estimate. No genetically encoded module, correlational selection or evolutionary convergence is established.
