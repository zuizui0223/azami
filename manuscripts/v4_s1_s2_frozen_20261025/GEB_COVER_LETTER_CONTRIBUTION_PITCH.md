## Proposed contribution paragraphs

Our Research Article, “Global photographic phenomics reveals climate-associated capitulum traits and local floral resemblance in Cirsium”, examines the climatic organisation of geographical variation and resemblance in reproductive appearance. It brings together climatic gradients among native taxa, colour variation within species ranges, and local multivariate resemblance across taxa. Continuous, geographically resolved measurements from public photographs connect these ecological comparisons.

The photographic framework retains eight constructs of capitulum presentation, colour and outline together with the identity and location of each photographic record. A common cohort of 156,683 photographs across 174 taxa demonstrates this capacity for joint measurement. The resulting data allow traits to be examined within sampled taxon distributions and permit local multivariate comparisons across taxa, while retaining the different eligibility requirements of individual analyses.

The three comparisons reveal climatic correspondence at complementary levels. Among native taxa, wetter warm-quarter climates accompany more downward-looking photographic capitula. Within the ranges of two widespread species, radiation or elevation is associated with floral colour. At climate-matched cells, taxa assigned to different longitude sectors also show greater joint capitulum resemblance than expected after complete trait vectors are reassigned within each taxon. This final comparison preserves the sampled cell-level multivariate distributions, demonstrating that their geographical arrangement contributes information beyond those distributions. The exploratory effect is positive at both 1° and 5°, with more stable statistical support at 5°. Rain protection, photoprotection and radiative warming provide distinct functional hypotheses for the observed gradients, supported by published experiments and comparative studies and presented as processes to test in Cirsium.

The article contributes to GEB's interest in broad ecological and biogeographical questions by connecting individual trait gradients with local multivariate resemblance. Its methodological value lies in preserving the information needed for this comparison; its ecological value lies in showing climatic organisation of reproductive appearance at occupied locations. Spatial-grain and coverage sensitivities define the present evidence, and the framework identifies climate-matched populations for calibrated field tests of the processes underlying that resemblance.

The pitch makes no claim of a new detection algorithm, independently calibrated V4 measurement accuracy, tested native–introduced slope differences, or demonstrated adaptation or evolutionary convergence. The comparison with taxon distributions follows the H3c permutation design, which preserves the eight selected cell-level vectors per taxon within each rarefaction; no predictive superiority benchmark against species means was performed. Taxa are assigned by their dominant longitude sector, and their individual matched cells need not occupy different sectors. Supplementary breadth, centroid, covariance and KDE results retain their original numerical results and correction families.
