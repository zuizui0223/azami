## Proposed interest paragraph

Our Research Article, “Global photographic phenomics reveals climate-associated capitulum traits and local floral resemblance in Cirsium”, connects geographical trait gradients with local multivariate resemblance across taxa. This addresses a general biogeographic question: whether similar climatic settings accompany similar reproductive phenotypes despite differences in taxonomic identity. We retain eight continuous constructs of capitulum presentation, colour and outline together with photographic-record identity and location; the common measurement cohort comprises 156,683 photographs across 174 taxa. Three complementary comparisons reveal climatic organisation of reproductive appearance. Among native taxa, wetter warm-quarter climates accompany more downward-looking capitula; within species ranges, radiation or elevation is associated with floral colour; and across 63 taxa, locally comparable climates accompany greater joint resemblance in angle, chroma and aspect than expected from reassignment of sampled multivariate trait vectors within taxa. The last comparison identifies information in the geographical arrangement of phenotypes beyond their sampled distributions. Rain protection, photoprotection and thermal effects provide functional hypotheses for subsequent field tests. The paper combines an ecological finding about the organisation of floral variation with a measurement framework that preserves continuous values, joint occurrence and place. Its relevance to GEB lies in extending broad-scale floral comparisons from individual-trait gradients to where similar combinations occur, with explicit taxonomic, photographic and spatial limits.
