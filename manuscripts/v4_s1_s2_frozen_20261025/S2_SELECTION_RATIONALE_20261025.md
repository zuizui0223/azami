# S2 focal-species eligibility and historical comparison

The rationale is a **two-stage intersection**, not merely having two invasive species:

1. **Conceptual:** the same plant species has a relatively long-established native range and a human-mediated secondary range; invasion-ecology theory proposes comparing photographed **trait–environment** relationships across those histories without assuming adaptive radiation or post-introduction genetic evolution (Hulme & Barrett, 2013; Colautti & Lau, 2015).
2. **Empirical feasibility:** 350 original source species names → seven with reliable wild WCVP native **and** introduced status → only *C. arvense* and *C. vulgare* with ≥100 assessable angle records, ≥15 spatial cells and ≥10 photographers **in each range**. See [Table S3](SI_S2_FOCAL_SPECIES_SELECTION_AUDIT.md) for all seven species, not just the two positive choices.

The two candidates were initially specified before all-350 screening; the later full-source audit confirmed the exact same two satisfy the sampling gate. Thus the basis is **retrospective source-coverage validation**, not preregistration or outcome-selection by Holm P. The 2 species account for 156,608 of 156,750 explicitly introduced-wild source observations in this dataset (99.91%) but this reflects **dataset ascertainment**, not global introduced-thistle taxonomic representation.

## Manuscript implication

- Methods should report selection date/order and explicit per-range coverage gates; Introduction/Discussion should connect these gates to the comparative invasion-ecology rationale.
- S2's four native/introduced strata estimate the **original 128 within-stratum flower-trait–environment associations**, not range-to-range climate-niche shifts or evolutionary speed.
- Two source-rich taxa cannot establish a phylogenetically replicated genus-wide evolutionary pattern; original image-colour validity remains an independent limitation.
