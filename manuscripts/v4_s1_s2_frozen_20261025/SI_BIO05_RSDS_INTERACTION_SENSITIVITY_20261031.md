# Supplementary sensitivity: does BIO05 moderate the association between RSDS and photographic *Cirsium* traits?

**Purpose.** The observational links between climate and flower appearance may not be additive. To evaluate an explicit thermal-radiative moderation hypothesis, we compared the original frozen four-covariate regression with and without a maximum-warm-month temperature × solar radiation interaction (BIO05 × RSDS). We also compared a three-covariate specification without elevation against a three-covariate model plus the interaction, rather than treating the interaction as a substitute for elevation.

**Data and matching.** All four fits shared the same original all-12-environment-complete, outcome-eligible photographs and original weights. For the native-taxon S1 analysis, a separately standardised interaction of the **between-taxon BIO05 and RSDS mean components** was appended to the original between/within-taxon design. Its presence therefore tests *between-taxon* moderation only. Within the four S2 species × native/introduced strata, we appended a separately standardised product of the within-stratum BIO05 and RSDS covariates, retaining the identifiable month/year nuisance adjustments, observer-by-5°-cell reduction, equal-cell weighting and cell-cluster-robust uncertainty. Both interacting main effects were retained, as were BIO18 and the original elevation term where relevant.

**Multiplicity.** Circular hue was treated as a joint 2-d.f. sine/cosine endpoint. Interaction coefficients were tested across **eight photographic constructs in S1** and **eight constructs × four S2 strata = 32 tests**, with Holm family-wise correction separately for the three-predictor-plus-interaction and four-predictor-plus-interaction versions. These are **post-result exploratory families**, not part of the original S1 80- or S2 128-test corrected endpoints.

**Model interpretation.** The negative interaction in introduced *C. vulgare* indicates that the conditional association of radiation with photographed chroma becomes smaller at higher temperature in this within-range regression. In the introduced-range M4I model, the RSDS main coefficient is +0.114108 at a standardised BIO05 value of 0. After accounting for the separately standardised BIO05×RSDS product (product SD=1.684643), the **model-implied conditional RSDS slopes** are approximately +0.16661 at BIO05 z=−1 and +0.06160 at BIO05 z=+1. These are conditional regression contrasts, **not independent climate-bin estimates**; they do not establish supported joint observational coverage across these hypothetical covariate values. It does not show a causal heat×radiation response, UV absorption, pigment concentration, thermoregulation, or adaptive evolution; the native and introduced interaction slopes were **not directly compared using a formal three-way range interaction**. The other 31 S2 interactions and all eight S1 interactions did not survive their respective Holm adjustments, and must remain visible as non-supporting results.

**Why elevation is not interchangeable with this interaction.** On the identical original **185,162 S1 chroma photographs from 177 taxa**, the among-taxon RSDS main coefficient was −0.243085 without elevation, −0.254786 without elevation plus BIO05×RSDS, −0.026577 with original elevation, and −0.039001 with original elevation plus BIO05×RSDS. Thus adding the interaction to the elevation-free model did not eliminate the strong negative RSDS–chroma association; adding elevation did. Original source validation recovered the frozen S1 BIO18 angle coefficient +0.045446843 (168,907 photos/178 taxa) and all six original S2 colour coefficients and cohorts before this analysis.

**Fit diagnostics and inference.** Adding the interaction lowered photo-level WLS in-sample AIC by at least two for all nine S1 numerical coordinates and 27 of 36 S2 coordinates, but this is not robust independent evidence of a climatic mechanism: photographic records share taxa/regions and the AIC likelihood does not replace cluster-robust testing or spatial-block out-of-sample prediction. S1's between-taxon product is not a test of within-population plasticity. These observations do not establish causal terrain, photo illumination, climate, pollinator or genetic effects.
