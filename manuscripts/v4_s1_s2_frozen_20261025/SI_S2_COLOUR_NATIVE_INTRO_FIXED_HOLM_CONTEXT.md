## Why this comparison belongs to the current GEB paper

Exactly **three** S2 trait–environment associations passed the familywise Holm threshold, and **all three** came from *introduced* strata (one *C. arvense* and two *C. vulgare*). That is a real observation **about which tests crossed a stringent statistical threshold**, not evidence that the biological correlations are absent from the native range or that introduction caused novel environmental responsiveness. This table retrieves the **native-range counterparts from the same original S2 128-test family**, without new fitting, different variables, or a new rainfall-niche comparison. It is **not a new model**, and the three native-versus-introduced pairs are presented as descriptive *within-stratum* estimates, not cross-range interaction tests.

The `S2/four_bio18` test family comprises **128 biological tests** (eight image traits × four predictors × four species/range strata); each circular-hue 2-df outcome is counted once. S2 contains **zero native-range Holm-passing** and **three introduced-range Holm-passing** rows. At the weaker **BH** threshold, however, native-range strata have **three** supported rows, including the native *C. vulgare* chroma–radiation association (BH P = 0.017760). The original unadjusted 95% coefficient intervals are **not** multiple-test-adjusted confidence bands. The four-variable model was fixed **after** exploration of alternatives; Holm does not correct for all prior model search.

## Three *different* biological interpretations, not one introduced-only mechanism

1. **Possible change in colour–elevation association (*C. arvense*).** The native image-lightness slope estimate is weakly positive (+0.0563, interval includes zero); the introduced estimate is negative (−0.1737, Holm P = 0.0013). This sign difference is **descriptively noteworthy**. It motivates a hypothesis that the distribution of photographed floral colour across elevation differs between ranges, but it does not test a direct range×elevation slope interaction or establish adaptation, plasticity or introduction-induced pigment change.
2. **A largely retained colour–radiation gradient (*C. vulgare* chroma).** Native and introduced standardized chroma–RSDS coefficients are nearly the same (+0.1369 and +0.1345). The native raw P = 0.00083 and BH P = 0.01776 show that the native association is **not unsupported at every inferential level**, even though its Holm P = 0.1024. This evidence contradicts treating the introduced significant result as evidence that chroma–radiation coupling was acquired upon introduction.
3. **A potentially different colour component response (*C. vulgare* lightness).** Lightness–RSDS coefficients are both negative, with a more negative introduced-range point estimate (−0.1428 versus −0.0488). Their different Holm decisions cannot demonstrate a statistically significant between-range difference. Chroma and lightness are estimated from related photographed pixels and must not be counted as independent biological mechanisms.

**Technical and interpretive boundaries:** These are **separate, range-stratified regression coefficients**, not a direct native-versus-introduced interaction test; predictor scaling and environmental/photo support differ across strata, and sample sizes and camera/observer distributions are unequal. Photo colour depends on illumination, white balance and processing; climatic RSDS is not a reading of incident light at the instant of photography. Alternative mechanisms include environmental filtering of recorded phenotypes, phenotypic plasticity, sampling of different ancestral/genotypic subsets, population differentiation and image-acquisition biases. None is uniquely identified. A common-garden or ancestry-aware field comparison with standardized reflectance and explicit matched-slope tests would be a **future study**, not a result added to the 24 October V4 freeze.

## Ecological inference for GEB

The most defensible positive result is **heterogeneity in detectable flower-colour–environment association across species and photographic colour dimensions, with three Holm-surviving coefficients concentrated in introduced strata**. Rather than presenting "introduced-only significance" as evolutionary novelty, the six original coefficients show **both apparent conservation (*C. vulgare* chroma×radiation) and a candidate change in association structure (*C. arvense* lightness×elevation)**. This expands the scale-dependent phenotype organisation argument without comparing **native versus introduced precipitation niches**.

Relevant interpretation sources: Hulme & Barrett (2013), *Journal of Ecology* 101:68–77, DOI [10.1111/1365-2745.12009](https://doi.org/10.1111/1365-2745.12009) (trait–environment gradient patterns as hypothesis generation, not proof of evolution); Colautti & Lau (2015), *Molecular Ecology* 24:1999–2017, DOI [10.1111/mec.13162](https://doi.org/10.1111/mec.13162) (nonrandom source sampling); Laitly et al. (2021), *Ecology and Evolution* 11:4071–4083, DOI [10.1002/ece3.7307](https://doi.org/10.1002/ece3.7307) (photographic colour uncertainty). These papers **motivate interpretation**; they do not verify our colour phenotypes or assert a causal evolutionary response.
