Dear Editors-in-Chief,

Please consider our Research Article, “Climatic matching reveals local capitulum resemblance and trait-specific gradients in *Cirsium*”, for publication in *Global Ecology and Biogeography*.

Large geographical databases reveal associations between environment and plant traits, but often reduce reproductive appearance to a species mean or an individual character. Our study instead asks whether **the same combinations of visible floral characters** recur at climatically similar locations, beyond what would be expected from the trait distributions already observed within each taxon. We link jointly measured capitulum presentation, photographic corolla chroma and projected outline to their original geographic localities, using an annual five-axis climate definition and taxon-preserving permutation tests across globally sampled native thistles. We then examine how this local ecological pattern relates to the separate, larger-scale problem of native–introduced climatic occupancy in two widespread species. We find repeatable native-locality multivariate associations, distinct exploratory precipitation–orientation and precipitation-seasonality–chroma gradients, and a descriptive introduced-range chroma contrast in *C. vulgare* that does not imply universal divergence of entire floral trait distributions. The general contribution is to distinguish **local environmental organisation of multivariate reproductive appearance** from **range-level environmental occupancy and trait-specific differences**—a distinction relevant to comparative macroecology and biogeography beyond thistles. We deliberately distinguish geographical correlations from the still-untested functional and adaptive mechanisms.
